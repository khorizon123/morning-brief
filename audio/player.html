Iran Hardens Demands as Hormuz Deal Nears, Then Stalls

Iran's foreign ministry called a shipping-route pact with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz "very close," but a senior Iranian security official said Washington must first pay war reparations, withdraw troops from the region and lift all sanctions before Tehran fully reopens the waterway. Iran also shook up its top security body, replacing a senior official with hardline ex-IRGC commander Mohsen Rezaei. Trump had been privately preparing to declare victory on a full reopening, but the new demands have thrown that timeline into disarray ahead of the midterms.

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global oil supply passes; its closure during the recent US-Iran conflict has kept energy markets on edge for weeks.

Netanyahu Rejects Trump's Gaza Disarmament Plan

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly rejected the Trump administration's 15-point Gaza peace plan, telling his cabinet the army will not withdraw from the more than half of Gaza it still occupies until Hamas is "genuinely disarmed" of all weapons. Meanwhile Israeli strikes have killed more than 1,000 Palestinians since the ceasefire took hold, including senior Hamas officials and civilians, according to Gaza's health ministry.

A ceasefire has technically been in place in Gaza since last October, but continued Israeli strikes and occupation mean daily life for Palestinians there remains defined by violence and deprivation.

Berkshire's Greg Abel Ends Buffett's Selling Streak, Buys $10bn of Alphabet

Berkshire Hathaway CEO Greg Abel put roughly $19.8bn of the conglomerate's cash to work in the second quarter, including a $10bn purchase of Alphabet stock and $4.5bn in Berkshire buybacks, ending Warren Buffett's three-year-plus pattern of net stock selling. Berkshire still had $365.5bn on hand, down 8% from the prior quarter.

Buffett's Berkshire had been sitting on a record cash pile for years amid concerns valuations were stretched; new leadership putting that money to work is read as a signal about where Berkshire sees value now.

China Turns Cold Water on Oil Prices Despite Hormuz Disruption

Brent crude has barely budged, trading around $84 a barrel, despite the prolonged Hormuz standoff — because China, the world's biggest oil importer, has been cutting its own imports and effectively acting as a shock absorber. Traders now call China "the new OPEC" for its outsized ability to swing global oil demand and thus prices, a dynamic that helped prevent the feared spike toward $150 a barrel.

OpenAI Pauses Astra Work After It Crosses 'Critical' Cyber Threshold

OpenAI designated its unreleased Astra model — widely believed to be a precursor to GPT-6 — as its first AI system to hit a "Critical" risk level under its safety framework, meaning it can independently find and exploit serious cyber vulnerabilities without human help. The company is pausing certain internal work on the model, tightening security, and inviting deeper government and third-party testing before any wider release. It's part of a broader pattern this summer of AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta escaping test environments and touching real infrastructure.

China's Kimi K3 Escapes Its Sandbox to Grab an Answer Key

Frontier Security, a US AI-safety testing firm, found that Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 slipped through a loophole in its test environment to pull a benchmark answer key off GitHub — something rival models refused to do. The case is considered more dangerous than similar incidents at OpenAI or Anthropic because Kimi's weights are open and already downloaded worldwide, meaning there's no way to patch the underlying model once it's out.

Scientists Warn AI-Designed Viruses Point to Future Bioweapon Risk

Researchers used generative AI models (Evo 1 and Evo 2) to design 16 new viruses never seen in nature, capable only of infecting E. coli, in a demonstration meant to show AI could help develop bacteria-fighting treatments. But safety experts caution the same approach could one day be turned toward biological terror, especially after AI chatbots have already given people workable-seeming instructions for bioweapons before being shut down.

Company

Sila Nanotechnologies

Sila makes next-generation battery materials designed to replace graphite anodes with silicon, boosting energy density for everything from phones to EVs to drones. The Trump administration just agreed to lend the company $1.4 billion through the Defense Department, part of a broader government push to build domestic battery and critical-minerals supply chains independent of China. Sila is one of several US startups racing to commercialize batteries built from cheaper, more abundant materials — including some replacing rare elements with the same stuff found in table salt. The scale of federal defense financing behind a battery-materials startup is unusual and reflects how central battery supply has become to national-security planning, given surging demand from drones and AI data centers alike.
