World

Trump Pledges $5,000 Payout to Voters as Approval Sinks Ahead of Midterms

At the Republicans' first-ever midterm convention in Dallas, Trump told voters to "pretend that I am running" and promised every adult American $5,000 if the GOP keeps Congress — a plan that would cost over $1.3 trillion and require congressional approval he doesn't have. It comes as his net approval sits at -26 and Democrats accuse him of election gerrymandering after conflicting court rulings threw Missouri's congressional map into chaos.

Midterm conventions are unusual — parties haven't held one ahead of an off-year election in nearly 50 years. Trump isn't on the ballot, but Republicans are staking their House and Senate majority on his popularity.

Treasury's $6bn Buyback Disappoints, Sending Yields to Multi-Year Highs

The US Treasury said it would buy back up to $6bn in long-dated government debt in its first operation since Scott Bessent's expanded scheme — well short of the $8-10bn Wall Street expected. Ten-year yields climbed toward 4.85-5%, their highest in nearly three years, and the US-China borrowing-cost gap widened to a record as investors questioned whether Bessent's buyback push can actually contain costs.

Bessent launched the buyback program last month promising to "at least double" purchases to arrest rising borrowing costs; markets are now testing his credibility.

Oil Tops $100 Again as US-Iran and Saudi-Houthi Conflicts Intensify

Brent crude broke back above $100 a barrel for the first time since July after the US destroyed five Iranian oil tankers and Iran retaliated with missiles at Jordan and fire on two US Navy destroyers. Separately, Saudi Arabia has vowed retaliation against Houthi forces after their biggest attack on the kingdom in years injured 73 civilians, raising fears the 2022 Yemen truce is collapsing.

The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil and gas; ongoing US-Iran fighting there has been the main driver of this year's energy price spikes. The Houthi-Saudi conflict is a separate but related front tied to the broader regional war.

Apple Unveils $1,999 Foldable iPhone Duo in Ternus's First Event as CEO

Apple revealed the iPhone Duo, its first foldable phone and biggest smartphone redesign in nearly 20 years, starting at $1,999 when it launches October 23. It's the debut event for new CEO John Ternus, who pitched the Duo as solving the "two phones awkwardly stuck together" feel of rival foldables from Samsung and Huawei, which have been available for eight years without catching on. Apple also raised prices across the iPhone 18 lineup, partly reflecting a memory-chip supply crunch.

LIV Golf Files for Chapter 11 as Saudi Backing Evaporates

The Saudi-backed LIV Golf league formally filed for bankruptcy protection in New Jersey after its main backer, the Public Investment Fund, pulled financial support this year. Lawyers say LIV is racing over the next month to sign existing players to a restructured "LIV 2.0" for a 2027 season that would be largely owned by those same golfers, many of whom are also its biggest creditors. It's a rare case of a Saudi-backed sports venture failing to dislodge an incumbent league (the PGA Tour) despite years of enormous guaranteed contracts.

Jacob Coxon, who worked at both Anthropic and OpenAI, resigned publicly saying the labs are "gambling with our lives" by racing toward self-improving AI, predicting things could be "out of control" by the end of next year. OpenAI's Navier-Stokes 'Breakthrough' Disputed by Mathematicians

OpenAI said an internal model beat mathematicians to a proof of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, a $1 million Millennium Prize question about whether smooth 3D fluid dynamics can develop a finite-time singularity — producing both an analytical proof and a Lean formalization. But rival mathematicians pursuing the same solution allege OpenAI learned of their unpublished work and used its compute advantage to beat them to the prize, sparking a credit dispute that raises questions about how mathematical research norms hold up against AI labs' speed.

Meta Launches Muse, an Always-On Personal AI Agent

Meta introduced Muse, a personal AI agent powered by its Muse Spark model that books travel, sends emails and shops on a user's behalf, running inside a secure "Muse Secure VM" with a Sentinel agent overseeing its actions for safety. An encrypted "Muse Confidential VM" is coming soon. It's available now on iOS, Android and muse.ai in the US, part of Meta's push to turn its cheaper, near-frontier models into a consumer super-app rather than just a chatbot.

OpenAI's Own Researchers Now Supervise 3+ 'Agent-Workdays' a Day

OpenAI disclosed that its research staff now oversee roughly 3.14 agent-workdays of coding output per eight-hour shift, but the leverage comes at a cost: median daily inference spend per researcher has jumped from $14 to over $600. The figures suggest recent productivity gains are partly just paying for a computer that never sleeps rather than genuine efficiency gains — a nuance likely to matter as labs tout AI-driven research acceleration to investors ahead of IPOs.

Google DeepMind Maps All 9 Billion Possible Human Genetic Variants

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a 1-petabyte database predicting the regulatory effects of every one of the roughly 9 billion possible single-nucleotide variants in the human genome. The tool is aimed at helping researchers quickly assess which genetic mutations might affect gene regulation, a potentially major aid for disease research that would otherwise require slow, expensive lab experiments to test each variant's effect individually.

Company

Clay

Clay makes AI-driven sales and marketing tools that automate prospecting — identifying promising customers, personalizing outreach, and figuring out when and how to reach leads. It just raised $115 million at a $7.1 billion valuation, more than double its mark from last August, in a round led by Wellington Management, an investor known for backing companies seen as strong IPO candidates. Clay's customers include Airbnb, Anthropic and DoorDash, and its annualized revenue is on track to roughly double next year to around $240 million. One founder, Kareem Amin, describes the product as a "self-learning engine" — notably, despite the IPO-friendly investor base, the company insists it isn't actively planning to go public, even as it's run as though that day will come.
