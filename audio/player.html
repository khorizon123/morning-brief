Trump and Xi Wrap State Visit With Pomp but Little Substance

Xi Jinping's three-day US state visit ended with a trip to the National Archives after a lavish White House dinner attended by tech titans like Bezos, Musk, Altman and Huang. The two sides extended their trade truce by just two months, to the end of January, with Washington refusing a longer deal because it says China hasn't upheld its side. Behind the scenes, the US pressed China on suspected clandestine low-level nuclear tests, which Beijing denies, while Xi publicly warned against falling into a "Thucydides trap" over Taiwan.

Xi's visit was his first to Washington in over a decade. The trade truce stems from a deal struck in Busan, South Korea last year amid the ongoing US-China tariff standoff.

Oracle's Force Majeure Notice on New Mexico Data Center Rattles AI-Infrastructure Financing

Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the Blue Owl-backed developer of Project Jupiter, a $165bn New Mexico data center meant to supply compute to OpenAI as part of Stargate. The notice — a move more common in mining and commodities to shield against geopolitical or climate risk — could let Oracle delay payments if the facility, expected to open in 2028, is held up by permitting fights over a natural gas pipeline hookup. Shares in Oracle and Blue Owl fell sharply on the news, and Oracle's 2056 bonds saw yields surpass 8%. The episode underscores how the AI buildout is colliding with local permitting fights and skittish credit markets even as demand for compute remains intense.

30-Year Mortgage Rate Tops 7% for First Time in Nearly Two Years

Freddie Mac data show the average 30-year US mortgage rate has crossed 7%, its highest since 2023, driven by costly oil and Treasury yields sitting near multi-decade highs. The 10-year Treasury yield hit roughly 5.13-5.17% this week, the highest since 2007, while 30-year yields touched levels last seen in 2004. The jump threatens to further chill an already-stagnant US housing market — August existing-home sales hit their lowest level in over a year — and adds to affordability pressure that could weigh on Republicans in the midterms.

SoftBank Prices Record $11bn Bond Sale to Fund Further OpenAI Investment

SoftBank sold roughly $11bn of dollar- and euro-denominated bonds — one of the largest high-yield debt deals ever — with some tranches carrying among the steepest yields the company has ever offered, to finance a follow-on investment in OpenAI. It's the third bond sale Masayoshi Son's conglomerate has done this year, following a ¥1trn ($6.4bn) issuance and earlier raises in April and June, reflecting how much debt is being layered under the AI buildout.

Meta's Muse Sparks E-Commerce Turf War, With Amazon Blocking the Agent

Meta's Muse AI shopping/chat agent has racked up about 3.4 million downloads since its Sept. 8 launch — faster early growth than Grok or Claude — and Meta used its Connect event to unveil Charm, a wearable keychain device for talking to Muse, plus a coming integration into its AI glasses and a "private processing" mode that shields data even from Meta. Walmart, Best Buy, Sephora, Instacart, Expedia and OpenTable are all partnering with Muse, but Amazon has blocked the agent from shopping on its site, protecting its sponsored-listing revenue. Analysts say Amazon risks losing customers to rivals like Walmart that embrace agentic shopping instead.

Google Puts AI Chips Into Orbit to Test Space-Based Data Centers

Google will launch Trillium TPUs into orbit next week aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 mission alongside satellite firm Planet, part of Project Suncatcher, a moonshot testing whether solar-powered satellites could one day host AI data centers. Satellites in the right orbit can capture roughly 8x more solar energy than ground installations, though heat dissipation in a vacuum remains a major engineering hurdle. If a two-satellite 2027 test succeeds, Google envisions laser-linked clusters of TPU-carrying satellites; rivals SpaceX (via SpaceXAI), Blue Origin and Starcloud are pursuing similar ideas. Skeptics, including OpenAI's Sam Altman, doubt orbital compute can scale this decade.

Company

Parallax

Parallax is a California startup using 3D printing to manufacture small, gas-fired turbines — a scrappy alternative to the specialty supply chains that make traditional turbines. With multiyear backlogs plaguing major turbine manufacturers amid surging AI-driven power demand, Parallax's pitch is smaller, cheaper, faster-to-build units (around 10 megawatts) that can be shipped in standard containers rather than needing custom heavy-industrial logistics. The company has raised $117 million to chase this niche. Its founder, Carl Schoeller, is explicit that Parallax isn't trying to out-engineer giants like GE or Siemens on efficiency — it's betting that speed and simplicity win in a market desperate for on-site power right now, particularly for data centers building their own generation to bypass grid bottlenecks.','
