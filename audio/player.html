Niger Coup Attempt Rattles Niamey

Mutinying soldiers launched attacks on Niger's capital Niamey and briefly seized the state television station, in what the government has called a coup attempt. Details on the ringleaders and the current status of the presidency remain unclear.

Congressman Pitches Tax on AI Tokens to Fund Displaced Workers

Texas Rep. Greg Casar, who chairs the Congressional Progressive Caucus, has proposed taxing AI companies on whichever is higher — their token usage or revenue — with rates that rise if AI causes significant job losses. The money would fund a new Work Protection Administration rather than a universal basic income, which Casar calls 'dystopic.' Bill Gates recently floated a similar token-tax idea. Casar argues a token tax, unlike a corporate tax hike, scales automatically with the scale of AI-driven disruption, and warns AI could become a top-tier issue in the 2028 presidential race.

AI Music Goes Mainstream, and Labels Are Split on What to Do About It

Suno, the AI music generator now valued at $5.4bn, says over 100 million people have used it to make songs, with about a dozen AI-assisted tracks reaching the Billboard charts and Deezer reporting AI songs now make up half its uploads. Comedian Bill Stiteler's Suno-made 'Puerto Rico Song' has racked up nearly 20 million Spotify streams and a licensing deal — though the streaming version is now a human remake. Warner settled its copyright suit against Suno and struck a deal letting artists opt in to AI versions of their voice and catalog, while Universal and Sony are still litigating; BMG has a similar licensing pact. Bandcamp, by contrast, has banned AI music outright.

Company

Suno

Suno is a Cambridge, Massachusetts-based AI music generator that lets anyone type a prompt and get a fully produced song back — vocals, instruments and all. Founded in 2022, it's raised roughly $1bn from backers including Bond Capital and Union Square Ventures at a $5.4bn valuation, and claims more than 100 million users. It's been sued by major labels (Warner settled and now licenses its catalog to Suno; Universal and Sony are still fighting in court), and roughly a dozen Suno-assisted tracks have already cracked the Billboard charts. Surprisingly, its CEO frames the mission not as disrupting music but democratizing 'creative fulfillment' — even as a hacker's leak of old training data reignited accusations that it scraped copyrighted songs without permission.
