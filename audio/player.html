Bond Sell-Off Deepens as 10-Year Yield Nears 5%, Oil Tops $109

The global bond rout intensified overnight: the US 10-year Treasury yield hit its highest since 2023 (around 4.95-4.96%), the 30-year mortgage rate crossed 7% for the first time in a year, and the S&P 500 fell for a fourth straight day. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's enhanced bond-buyback program bought only $5.19bn of long-dated debt, well below the roughly $8-10bn Wall Street expected, disappointing investors hoping for a bigger intervention. Brent crude spiked as high as $109 a barrel before settling near $104, driven by fears the Strait of Hormuz conflict and new Red Sea disruptions will keep squeezing energy supply.

Rising government bond yields raise borrowing costs across the economy — mortgages, corporate debt, credit cards — and are historically bad for stock valuations. Markets are also awaiting a closely watched US inflation report that could sway the Fed's coming rate decision.

Houthis Seize Strategic Red Sea Port and Island, Tightening Grip on Bab el-Mandeb

Iran-backed Houthi rebels captured the Yemeni port city of Mokha and, in a rapid follow-on offensive, seized Perim Island and the town of Dhubab — giving them effective control of both channels of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, one of the world's most critical shipping chokepoints. Saudi Arabia's oil output has fallen 23% to its lowest level since 1990 as the kingdom's west-coast export routes come under threat, and OPEC cut its global oil-demand growth forecast. Roughly 12-15% of global maritime trade, worth more than $1 trillion a year, passes through the strait.

The Bab el-Mandeb connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and is a vital artery for oil and container shipping between Asia, Europe and the Middle East; a blockage there compounds pressure from the ongoing US-Iran conflict near the Strait of Hormuz.

Israel Destroys Major Hezbollah Tunnel System in Southern Lebanon

Israel's military said it blew up a Hezbollah tunnel complex under the Ali al-Taher ridge, which it claims extends more than 2km, following a months-long campaign to seize the strategic site overlooking southern Lebanon. Iran had previously warned it would respond forcefully to any Israeli offensive there.

The ridge sits on the Israel-Lebanon border and offers a commanding view of southern Lebanon; Israel occupied the area last week as part of efforts to degrade Iran-backed Hezbollah's military infrastructure.

Nvidia's Groq Deal Draws Justice Department Antitrust Scrutiny

The Justice Department has opened an investigation into Nvidia's December partnership with chipmaker Groq, examining whether the 'nonexclusive licensing agreement' — under which Nvidia also hired away Groq's top executives — was really a disguised acquisition designed to dodge antitrust review. Regulators are increasingly wary of these 'acqui-hire' deals, which let tech giants absorb a startup's key talent and assets without a formal merger review; FTC chair Andrew Ferguson has flagged the trend publicly. If the DOJ finds fault, Nvidia would likely face a fine rather than having to unwind the deal. Notably, the probe shows that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's close relationship with President Trump hasn't shielded the company from scrutiny.

Trump's Proposed $5,000-Per-Adult 'Dividend' Faces Fiscal and Political Hurdles

President Trump pledged at the GOP midterm convention to send every adult American a $5,000 check if Republicans keep control of Congress — a plan that would cost roughly $1.3 trillion, more than annual US defense spending. Bond markets so far aren't panicking, largely because investors doubt the promise will materialize, echoing prior unfulfilled Trump giveaway pledges. But the proposal complicates Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's efforts to reassure markets that Washington is serious about controlling the deficit, especially after a disappointing bond-buyback operation this week failed to arrest the yield surge. Even fiscal-hawk Republicans have pushed back on the idea.

Apple's iPhone Duo Debuts to Mixed-but-Bullish Reviews

Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo, its first foldable phone and biggest smartphone redesign in nearly two decades, starting at $1,999 and launching October 23 — the debut 'hero' product under new CEO John Ternus. Analysts are more bullish than expected: Deepwater's Gene Munster doubled his revenue-share forecast to 10% of FY27 iPhone sales, and other watchers say Apple will sell every unit it can make. Key risks include the device being 29% thicker and 25% heavier than the iPhone 17 Pro, a global memory-chip shortage limiting supply, and possible cannibalization of iPad sales given its Apple Pencil support.

Private Equity Piles Into 'Experience Economy' Fitness Brands Like Hyrox

A group including private equity firm L Catterton is investing in Hyrox, the German-founded fitness-racing competition, valuing the Hamburg business at nearly $700 million; former DreamWorks Animation CEO Jeffrey Katzenberg is also part of the investor group via his WndrCo firm. It's part of a broader PE rush into 'experience economy' businesses seen as AI-resistant — Apollo started a $6bn sports fund and bought into the Yankees, while Ari Emanuel's MARI agreed to buy theater company ATG Entertainment for around $6bn. Hyrox, where participants alternate 1km runs with strength exercises, expects two million participants this year and is eyeing a bigger apparel business and televised events, with founder Christian Toetzke hoping it eventually becomes an Olympic sport.

Anthropic Details Global Misuse of Claude, Including Weapons and Espionage Cases

Anthropic published a 150-plus-page threat report detailing eight months of blocked misuse of its Claude models, including scientists probing possible bioweapons applications, a Yemen-based actor using Claude Code to build rocket guidance software, and a consultant building Mali's spy agency a surveillance system targeting 25 million phone lines. Separately, the FT reports Houthi militants used Claude to help develop guidance and control software for ballistic missiles. Anthropic also named seven Chinese AI labs — including Alibaba, DeepSeek, Moonshot and Xiaomi — accused of 'distilling' Claude via fraudulent accounts, with Moonshot and DeepSeek allegedly passing off Claude's outputs to customers as their own. The disclosures landed as OpenAI's Sam Altman told staff the company is weighing whether to voluntarily slow model development, following a warning from OpenAI's chief scientist that labs should coordinate on pausing.

DeepSeek Ships V4.1-Flash, Undercutting Rivals on Price Again

Chinese lab DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash, an efficient open-weight model that reportedly beats Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on several agentic, coding and cybersecurity benchmarks despite running at roughly a quarter of the per-token cost of DeepSeek's own prior flagship. Pricing comes in at just $0.15/$0.60 per million tokens, and DeepSeek published the weights on Hugging Face under an MIT license. The model uses an asymmetric architecture and smaller KV cache to boost intelligence-per-dollar, joining Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash as one of the strongest cheap options on the market — underscoring how Chinese labs are squeezing margins across the industry just as usage grows more price-sensitive.

Apple's Siri AI Launches in Limited Beta With Usage Caps

Apple's long-delayed Siri AI overhaul will remain in beta when it ships with iOS 27 on September 14, arriving with daily usage caps, language and regional restrictions, and the prospect of paid tiers for expanded access down the road. Limits will vary by feature, request complexity and Apple's available server capacity — a sign the company is still cautious about compute costs and reliability even as rivals race ahead with agentic assistants. It's a notably more conservative rollout than Meta's Muse or OpenAI's ChatGPT Work, reflecting Apple's history of delays on this project.

Arini Capital Management

Arini is a London hedge fund managing $22bn that specializes in Europe's thorniest distressed-debt trades, taking large, leveraged, concentrated bets against struggling corporates and their creditors. Founded in 2021 by former Credit Suisse trader Hamza Lemssouguer, its contrarian approach delivered blockbuster returns — up 27% in 2023, 21% in 2024, 10% last year — but its flagship fund is now down more than 8% in 2026 after bruising bets on Aston Martin and Patrick Drahi's Altice International both went sour. Surprisingly, this isn't Arini's first big drawdown: it lost 15% in its first months after launching in 2022, then rebounded roughly 29% the following year, and investors are watching to see if history repeats.
