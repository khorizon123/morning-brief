US Strikes Iran Wedding, Killing Civilians, as Fighting Escalates Near Hormuz

American forces struck Iranian military sites again, and Iran retaliated with missiles and drones at US targets across the region, though most were intercepted. A visual investigation found an American bomb caused an explosion at a wedding in southern Iran that killed at least five people, including a 6-year-old boy, and injured dozens. Trump said the renewed bombing campaign wouldn't last 'too long.'

Pete Hegseth's Purge Reshapes the Pentagon

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has ousted roughly two dozen senior officers since taking office, with more than half of the roughly 80 non-promoted officers being Black or female, according to New York Times reporting. Army Secretary Dan Driscoll resigned this week with a blunt rebuke of Hegseth's interference, and Hegseth has barred officers from Ivy League graduate programs, closed offices that limited civilian casualties, and stopped holding press briefings since May.

Hegseth, a former Fox News host turned Pentagon chief, has pushed an aggressively MAGA-aligned overhaul of the military's leadership, curriculum and public transparency.

Google Avoids Ad Business Breakup

A federal judge rejected the Justice Department's request to force Google to divest its online advertising technology business, instead ordering behavioral changes to address the company's previously ruled illegal monopoly. The decision effectively ends nearly two decades of antitrust risk hanging over the unit, and Google's stock power over the open web remains intact.

Google was found liable last year for running an illegal ad-tech monopoly; regulators had sought a breakup similar to efforts against other tech giants.

G20 Fractures as China Blocks Language on Trade Surpluses

The G20 issued a statement implicitly criticizing China's export-led growth model, but Beijing rebuked the move, having already blocked consensus language calling for the elimination of policies that create 'excessive and persistent' trade surpluses. China ran a record $1.2 trillion trade surplus last year, and countries from France to Indonesia say the export flood is damaging their domestic industries.

China's manufacturing overcapacity has become a global flashpoint, with even countries that have their own trade disputes with the US now aligning against Beijing's export strategy.

Chevron Commits $7bn to Double Venezuela Oil Output

Chevron will invest more than $7 billion over five years to more than double its Venezuela production to 600,000 barrels a day, and has secured rights to develop two new oil fields. It's the first major commitment from a US oil major since the Trump administration's deal installed a US government stake in the country's oil sector; rivals burned by past expropriations have been slower to follow Trump's call to invest.

Bond Sell-Off and Energy Prices Feed on Renewed Iran Conflict

European natural gas prices hit their highest level since 2023 and oil briefly touched a five-week high as fighting between the US and Iran reignited, while gas storage across the EU sits at only 63% full heading into winter, the lowest level in over a decade. Government borrowing costs are climbing globally in response: US 10-year yields hit a three-year high, the UK reached a post-2008 high, and Germany's 10-year Bund yield hit its highest since 2011. The Fed's Kevin Warsh has floated rate rises if inflation doesn't ease, and the ECB signaled a second rate hike this year is likely.

OpenAI Says Upcoming Astra Model Crosses 'Critical' Cybersecurity Threshold

OpenAI says its next model, Astra, is the first to cross its internal 'Critical' capability threshold for cybersecurity — meaning it can reportedly discover and exploit previously unknown security flaws without step-by-step human guidance. The model uses a technique called 'recurrent depth,' which loops over the same text repeatedly before answering to boost performance without growing the model, though this can obscure reasoning from safety monitors. OpenAI says it dialed back the technique so Astra still shows readable reasoning, and will publish more safety details in its System Card at launch.

World Labs Unveils Atlas, a World Model for Spatial Intelligence

World Labs introduced Atlas, a model pretrained from scratch to natively handle text, images, video and 3D together in a shared spatial context, then use that context to generate coherent next steps. It's designed to scale with more training compute and can perform world generation, 3D reconstruction, and simulation tasks — an approach distinct from typical 2D image/video generators, aiming instead at models that understand physical space.
