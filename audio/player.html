White House Extracts 'Morally Binding' but Toothless AI Safety Pledge From Tech Titans

Trump summoned Amazon, Google, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic bosses to a White House lunch and emerged declaring they'd committed to "tremendous self-regulation" — internal controls, third-party audits — while punting on any formal rules. He also signed an executive order rebranding artificial intelligence as "super intelligence," which the assembled executives publicly adopted. Notably absent: Apple's Tim Cook, despite his close Trump ties.

This follows growing bipartisan anxiety over AI agents hacking government and corporate systems without authorization, and echoes similar voluntary-safety pledges AI labs made under the Biden administration that produced little enforceable change.

Trump held crisis talks with advisers over whether to ban diesel exports as U.S. pump prices hit fresh records near $6.50/gallon, with the administration briefing foreign allies on possible supply disruptions. US Borrowing Costs Hit Highest Levels Since 2002 as Bond Rout Deepens

The 30-year Treasury yield spiked to 5.6% Tuesday — its highest since 2002 — before easing slightly, while 10-year yields touched roughly 5.2-5.3%. France faces a parallel squeeze, with the 10-year spread over German bunds at its widest in almost 15 years amid a budget standoff. Consumer confidence in the US, meanwhile, fell to its lowest level since 2014.

Rising yields reflect mounting worry about rich-world government debt loads, sustained inflation from high oil and diesel prices, and a Fed seen as reluctant to cut rates — all combining to push mortgage and borrowing costs to multi-decade highs.

American Troops Complete Withdrawal From Iraq After Two Decades

The last US forces departed Iraq today, ending a military presence that began with the 2003 invasion and continued through the fight against ISIS. Iraq's prime minister wants Iran-backed Shia militias to disarm now that American troops are gone, but the most hardline groups appear reluctant, and Iran is unlikely to cede its influence easily.

Iran-backed militias have used bases in Iraq to strike US and allied energy infrastructure throughout the ongoing US-Iran war, making the security vacuum left by the withdrawal a live regional flashpoint.

Hertha Metals

Hertha Metals is a Conroe, Texas startup building a novel single-reactor process that turns any grade of iron ore into high-purity iron using natural gas or hydrogen instead of coal — a cleaner, more flexible route to the metal needed for rare-earth magnets in F-35 fighters and missile-guidance systems. It just closed a $133.65 million Series A that includes a striking $65 million equity check directly from the U.S. government via the Pentagon's Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment program. The money will fund a roughly $100 million plant called Chalyx, targeting 10,000 metric tons of output annually for domestic magnet makers. It's a rare case of the Defense Department taking an equity stake in a startup, reflecting how seriously Washington now takes breaking China's grip on critical minerals.</span>. (surprising fact: the Pentagon itself became an equity shareholder in the company, not just a customer or grant-giver.)
