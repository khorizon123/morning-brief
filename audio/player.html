US-Iran Truce Holds Into Fourth Day as Trump Signals Openness to Talks

Fighting between the US and Iran paused for a fourth consecutive night, with Trump saying there was "a good chance" negotiations with Tehran could produce a lasting peace deal — though Iranian officials sounded far less optimistic. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz stayed thin over the weekend (six to twelve crossings a day) even with the lull, and war-risk insurance for a single large tanker transiting the strait has topped $10 million.

The US and Iran have been trading strikes for weeks in a war that spiked oil prices above $100 a barrel; Oman has been mediating on rerouting tanker traffic. Markets are watching closely because any breakdown could reignite the oil-driven inflation scare that's been rattling bond markets.

Wildfires Force Evacuations Above 300,000 as France Braces for New Heat Wave

France and Spain continued battling what officials call the region's worst wildfire season in modern history, with more than 300,000 people evacuated and blazes advancing near Bordeaux and Madrid. Macron called the fires France's "most difficult situation" since World War II, and forecasters warn a fresh heat wave and strong winds this week could intensify the flames further. The fires have generated "pyrocumulonimbus" fire-thunderstorms — a phenomenon never before recorded in France — which spark their own lightning and erratic winds.

Pyrocumulonimbus clouds form when a fire burns hot enough to generate its own thunderstorm system, making blazes far harder to predict and fight.

Markets & Deals

AI Chip Rout Deepens as SK Hynix, Samsung Plunge; Apple Retakes World's Most Valuable Crown

A global chip stock sell-off intensified as South Korea's Kospi plunged more than 10% (triggering a trading halt), Samsung fell 12-14%, and Kioxia in Tokyo dropped 18%. SK Hynix and Samsung have now tumbled 41% and 34% respectively in July. The rout, which also knocked $250 billion off Nvidia's value and let Apple reclaim the title of world's most valuable public company, is driven by three fears: unsustainable AI capex, circular vendor financing between chipmakers and AI labs, and rising Chinese competition (Alibaba, Moonshot's Kimi K3). Investors are bracing for earnings this week from Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Apple, SK Hynix and Samsung for clues on when AI spending will pay off.

Nvidia Emerges as AI's Central Banker: $50bn Texas Lease, $250bn OpenAI Backstop Talks

Nvidia disclosed it has signed leases worth up to $50 billion for a massive Texas data center that will run on its own chips, and is reportedly in talks to guarantee OpenAI roughly $250 billion in financing to cover lease costs on a $500 billion Ohio data center campus. Nvidia also agreed to invest $5 billion in Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence. Critics say this vendor-financing pattern — chipmakers bankrolling the very AI labs that buy their chips — echoes the circular financing that inflated and then popped the 2001 telecom bubble, even though similar hardware-titan-backed credit built out America's electrical grid and broadband. Hyperscalers are set to borrow up to $400 billion this year alone for AI infrastructure, unnerving bond investors already jumpy about inflation.

CXMT Soars 466% in Shanghai's Biggest IPO Since 2010

Chinese memory-chip maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) surged roughly 466% on its Shanghai debut, briefly becoming mainland China's most valuable listed company at a valuation near $487-547 billion — the country's biggest IPO since Agricultural Bank of China in 2010. CXMT makes DRAM chips used in servers and phones, supplies about 11% of global DRAM wafer capacity, and has been courted by Apple despite US politicians pushing to restrict American firms from buying Chinese memory chips. The rally represents a milestone in Beijing's push to build an AI supply chain insulated from US export controls, and comes as Chinese AI hardware more broadly rattles Western markets.

J&J had offered $8 billion in 2024 before a judge rejected that settlement; the lower offer this time came after a different judge threatened to dismiss the cases outright for lack of evidence, strengthening J&J's negotiating hand.

Ares in Talks to Acquire Leonard Green & Partners

Ares Management, the roughly $650 billion private-lending giant, has held discussions to acquire Leonard Green & Partners, one of the largest privately held PE firms with about $85 billion in assets. Ares currently has just $25 billion in private-equity strategies, so the deal would sharply expand its footprint in traditional buyouts and, potentially, retail-investor products — Leonard Green's stable of predictable-earnings businesses like PureGym and Authentic Brands is seen as a good fit for individual-investor offerings. AI & Tech

Moonshot Publishes Weights for Kimi K3, Largest Open Model Ever Released

Moonshot AI officially released the weights and technical report for its 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3, letting anyone with sufficient GPU power download and run the model — the biggest open-weight release in history. K3 has already been competing with Claude and GPT-5.6-Sol on benchmarks, and Moonshot also opened core infrastructure pieces like attention kernels and agent tooling under a license that permits commercial hosting. US officials have accused Moonshot of building K3 by illicitly distilling outputs from American models, a charge Beijing rejects as "AI hegemony." The release intensifies the fight over whether Washington should restrict open-weight AI, with Anthropic's Dario Amodei clarifying that his company doesn't support an outright ban but wants chip controls and anti-distillation rules instead.

It reportedly leads several coding and knowledge-work benchmarks. The launch comes as Anthropic navigates the broader open-vs-closed AI fight, with CEO Dario Amodei publicly distancing the company from calls to ban open-weight models while pushing instead for chip export controls and distillation crackdowns.

Nvidia Leads 50-Company Alliance Pushing Open-Weight AI as a Security Asset

Nvidia's Jensen Huang posted a letter — now signed by around 50 companies including Microsoft, Meta, SpaceX, Palantir, and, notably, new additions OpenAI and Google — arguing that open-weight AI models strengthen cybersecurity and innovation rather than threatening it. The group also launched the Open Secure AI Alliance to share technical safeguards and defensive research. The debate has sharpened after last week's disclosure that Hugging Face's own defenses couldn't rely on secretive closed frontier models for help during a hack; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has meanwhile threatened Chinese AI firms with sanctions if they cross into IP theft via distillation.

Company

Ares Management

Ares is a roughly $650 billion Los Angeles-based alternative asset manager best known as one of Wall Street's premier private-lending shops, with a credit arm that rivals Blackstone, Apollo and KKR. It's suddenly interesting because it's reportedly in talks to acquire Leonard Green & Partners, an ~$85 billion private-equity firm behind PureGym and Authentic Brands — a deal that would nearly quadruple Ares's private-equity assets overnight. Despite its scale, private equity makes up only about 4% of Ares's total assets under management, a striking imbalance for a firm its size. Ares has been acquisitive before, buying property manager GLP's non-China unit in 2024 and secondaries specialist Landmark Partners in 2021. The Leonard Green courtship comes amid a broader wave of consolidation among alternative-asset managers, as firms like GIP, HPS and Castlelake have already sold to bigger rivals rather than fight a tough fundraising market alone.'
