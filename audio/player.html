Iran Attacks US Forces in Jordan, Ending Days-Long Pause

Iran launched a surprise ballistic-missile attack on American forces in Jordan; all missiles were intercepted. The US and Saudi Arabia responded with joint strikes on Iran-aligned militias in Iraq. The attack ends a fragile days-long truce and puts fresh pressure on Trump to resume the broader air campaign against Iran.

The US and Iran have been trading strikes for weeks in a war that began over Iran's nuclear program and has since drawn in Gulf shipping lanes and regional militias.

Iran Rejects Oman's Hormuz Compromise, Demanding Control of Inbound Lane

Iran rejected an Omani proposal to split control of the Strait of Hormuz evenly, with a top Iranian diplomat insisting Tehran take the inbound shipping lane entirely and leave only part of the outbound lane to Oman. The rejection jeopardizes hopes of quickly resuming US-Iran negotiations and keeps a critical oil chokepoint in dispute.

The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global oil supply; its closure or contested control has been a central flashpoint of the US-Iran war.

Europe's Wildfires Advance on Madrid and Bordeaux Ahead of New Heat Wave

Fires in Spain and France continue to set modern-era records, with flames coming unsettlingly close to Madrid and threatening storied Bordeaux vineyards. A fresh heat wave with temperatures near 104°F was forecast to hit the region, complicating firefighting efforts already stretched thin by a shortage of Canadair water-bomber planes.

Last year was Europe's worst fire season on record; 2026's fires, fueled by the hottest June on record in Western Europe, are already outpacing that pace.

Japan Earthquake Kills at Least 13, Traps Dozens in Collapsed Mall

A 6.8-magnitude earthquake struck Japan's Kumamoto prefecture, igniting fires, damaging Kumamoto Castle, and collapsing part of a shopping mall where dozens of workers are feared trapped. More than 300,000 people were told to evacuate, and rescue crews are racing against time.

Japan sits on the Pacific "Ring of Fire" and has strict building codes, but a quake of this magnitude striking a populated area can still cause mass casualties and infrastructure damage.

UPS Raises Outlook as Restructuring, Amazon Pullback Pay Off

UPS lifted its full-year revenue outlook to about $91.2 billion, ahead of Wall Street forecasts, after cutting tens of thousands of driver and warehouse jobs and phasing out roughly half its lower-margin Amazon volume in favor of premium, small-business and healthcare shipments. Profit fell sharply on a large restructuring charge and higher fuel costs tied to the Iran war. The company is doubling down on RFID technology embedded in labels and delivery trucks. It's a rare bright spot in an otherwise shaky earnings season for industrials.

AI & Tech

1,000+ AI Lab Staffers Sign Letter Urging a 'Pacing' Mechanism for AI Progress

Over 1,000 employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and other frontier labs signed a "Pacing the Frontier" letter asking the US to help build tools that could deliberately slow AI progress if automated AI research starts improving models beyond human oversight. Signatories include Anthropic co-founders Jack Clark and Chris Olah and chief scientists from several labs. Unlike past open letters from outside academics, this one comes from inside the labs themselves, both OpenAI and Anthropic endorsed it publicly — a notable shift in tone from an industry usually racing full-speed.

Zuckerberg Tells Trump Administration Not to Ban Chinese AI Models

In a WSJ op-ed and FT interview, Mark Zuckerberg argued Washington should not block Chinese open-weight models like Moonshot's Kimi K3, warning that doing so would amount to "regulatory capture" by closed-model labs like OpenAI and Anthropic. He argues open access — not automation — is what will keep AI safe, predicting more jobs, not fewer, if the technology spreads widely. The remarks come as Trump officials weigh sanctions on Moonshot over alleged distillation of Anthropic's Claude, and as Meta itself drifts toward more closed models.

Moonshot Releases Kimi K3 Weights, History's Largest Open Model

Moonshot AI published the full weights and technical report for Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with native visual understanding, a 1-million-token context window, and a novel attention architecture Moonshot says delivers 2.5x the intelligence per unit of compute. The company is also open-sourcing supporting infrastructure — attention kernels, a MoE communication library, and agent-environment tooling. It's the biggest open-weight release yet and intensifies the debate over whether the US should restrict such Chinese models.

Company

Moonshot AI

Moonshot AI is the Beijing-based lab behind Kimi, whose latest model, Kimi K3, is now the largest open-weight AI model ever released — a 2.8-trillion-parameter system whose full weights anyone with sufficient GPU power can download and run for free. The company has rocketed to a $31.5 billion valuation on the strength of benchmarks that reportedly beat both Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's GPT-5.5. It's also become a geopolitical flashpoint: US officials have accused Moonshot of building K3 by covertly "distilling" knowledge out of Anthropic's Claude models, a claim that's fueling White House talk of sanctions even as tech giants like Meta and Nvidia publicly defend open-weight models as a security asset rather than a threat. The company is currently opening up not just the model but the surrounding toolchain — attention kernels, MoE communication libraries and agent infrastructure — an unusually generous move even by open-source standards.6
