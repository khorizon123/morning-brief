Bond Rout Deepens: 10-Year Treasury Hits Highest Since 2002

The 10-year US Treasury yield spiked to 5.34% before easing slightly, its highest since 2002, while UK 30-year gilts topped 6% and French 10-year yields hit a 2002-era high of 4.96%. The sell-off pushed the 30-year US fixed mortgage rate up to 7.28% from 7.03% — the biggest weekly jump in four years — and is raising financing costs across the AI data-center buildout, with some neocloud bond yields nearing 10%.

Rising government bond yields reflect investor worry about heavy borrowing by governments and AI companies alike; higher yields translate directly into higher mortgage and corporate debt costs.

Trump Weighs Diesel Export Ban as Global Supplies Tighten

Putin said Russia won't sell diesel abroad until sanctions are lifted, while Chinese refiners suspended October exports, squeezing a fuel that normally sees a third of global exports come from those two sources. The Trump administration is considering restricting or banning US diesel exports to curb domestic prices near $6.50/gallon, reportedly telling France and Germany to release their own reserves or face the ban, even as EU governments weigh releasing 50 million barrels of reserves themselves.

Diesel powers trucking, farming and shipping, so price spikes ripple through the entire economy; wars in Ukraine and the Middle East have throttled supply from two major exporting regions.

Google Extends AI Watermarking Into Biology With SynthID Bio

Google DeepMind introduced SynthID Bio, a proof of concept that adapts its AI watermarking technology to proteins by tweaking its AlphaFold 3 model to embed a hidden, detectable pattern in predicted protein shapes without hurting accuracy. The idea is to help DNA suppliers trace unfamiliar AI-designed sequences before fulfilling orders, potentially speeding up safety screening that currently requires manual review. Lab tests found watermarked proteins still functioned normally once produced, though Google acknowledges it offers no guarantee against tampering.

Company

Supabase

Supabase is an open-source database platform that lets developers — increasingly AI coding agents rather than humans — spin up databases in seconds instead of configuring servers from scratch. It just raised $150 million at a $10.65 billion valuation, barely months after a $500 million round, and is simultaneously acquiring rival Turso to help users and agents build cheap software prototypes. The company adds nearly five million new databases a month, about 70% of them created by AI tools rather than people, and serves more than 500,000 paying customers and 13 million developers. Surprisingly, despite its scale, a recent security audit found more than 16,000 of its hosted databases had exposed sensitive customer data, a reminder that the AI coding boom is outrunning basic security hygiene.
