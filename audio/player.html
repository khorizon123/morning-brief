Houthis' Red Sea Grip Forces Saudi Pipeline Shutdown, Oil Climbs Further

Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz following renewed attacks, adding fresh momentum to already-surging oil prices. The move follows the Houthis' capture of Yemen's Perim Island and Mokha, giving the Iran-backed group effective control of both channels of the Bab al-Mandeb strait. The US has separately narrowed air-defense windows for tankers transiting Hormuz to just two specified times daily as Iranian attacks increase.

Houthi rebels, backed by Iran, control large parts of Yemen and have used missiles and drone strikes to threaten shipping through one of the world's most important oil chokepoints. Roughly a tenth of global seaborne oil trade passes through this corridor.

South Korea Overhauls Espionage Law to Guard Chip Secrets

South Korea broadened its espionage legislation for the first time in more than 70 years, aiming to close gaps that let foreign spies target its semiconductor industry's trade secrets. The move underscores growing anxiety in Seoul over corporate espionage as chip technology becomes central to great-power competition.

South Korea is home to Samsung and SK Hynix, two of the world's most important memory-chip makers, making its tech IP a frequent target for state-linked industrial espionage.

Canada's Carney Courts $1tn From Investors to Decouple From Trump's US

Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted a summit aimed at attracting up to $1tn from investors seeking a haven from an increasingly hostile United States, part of his broader push to reduce Canada's economic dependence on its southern neighbor. The pitch comes as Trump has escalated the trade war, banning Canadian dairy, alcohol and motorcycles and threatening more tariffs after Canada retaliated on $20bn of US goods.

Trump has spent recent months ratcheting up tariffs and threats against Canada, prompting Ottawa to actively pursue new capital and trade partners outside the US as insurance against further disruption.

Company

Vy Capital

Vy Capital is a low-profile venture capital firm that has quietly built one of the largest outside stakes in SpaceX, now estimated around $40bn — making it one of Elon Musk's most important, least-scrutinized backers. Unlike splashy Silicon Valley VCs, Vy rarely courts press attention despite anchoring positions in some of the most sought-after private tech companies. The firm has also been an early or major backer of other high-profile names like Revolut and Nubank, giving it broad exposure across fintech and space simultaneously. Its SpaceX stake alone likely makes it one of the most successful venture bets of the decade, built almost entirely outside public view. Surprising fact: despite its outsized stakes, Vy Capital maintains almost no public-facing presence or communications team, a deliberate contrast to peers who compete for headlines.
