Kratom Overdoses Surge, Sparking Federal Crackdown

Kratom — a Southeast Asian tree-leaf extract sold legally at gas stations and smoke shops as a pain and energy remedy — is linked to a 12-fold rise in US poison-control reports over the past decade and roughly 5,200 fatal overdoses recorded by the CDC between 2020 and 2024. The compound binds to opioid receptors, and newer concentrated derivatives like 7-OH are far more potent than traditional powder forms. Federal, state and local regulators are moving to ban synthetic derivatives or the substance outright, while the kratom industry pushes back, arguing plain-leaf kratom is a safe alternative that can reduce dependence on opioids.

Robinhood Markets

Robinhood has spent the past few years transforming itself from a meme-stock trading app into a platform pushing retail investors deeper into territory once reserved for institutions and the ultra-wealthy — including, now, venture capital. Its second venture fund, Robinhood Ventures Fund II, just IPO'd on the NYSE, partnering with Y Combinator to give ordinary investors a stake in early-stage startups, following its first fund's bets on Stripe, Ramp and OpenAI. The surprising fact: Fund I's share price soared 250% within months of its debut before crashing back near its $25 offer price, illustrating just how volatile these retail vehicles can be despite marketing them as long-term access to 'the next Anthropic.' Critics, including a Harvard Business School professor quoted in the press, warn that everyday investors often don't fully grasp what they're buying into — but Robinhood is betting retail appetite for private-market exposure only grows from here.
