Canada Hits Back With Tariffs on $20bn of US Goods as Trump Threatens Bombardier

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly 700 American products — including steel, dairy and home appliances — took effect at midnight, matching the levies Trump imposed on Canadian goods after talks collapsed last month. Trump responded by threatening to bar Bombardier, Canada's business-jet maker, from selling in the US unless it builds planes domestically, and floated halting bilateral trade outright. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent dismissed Canada as a 'little yippy dog' antagonizing a 'German Shepherd,' while 75% of Canadians polled back PM Mark Carney's hard line.

Carney took office promising to be tough on Trump and walked away from trade talks on August 21st. The dispute has scrambled a historically close alliance, with Canada diversifying trade away from the US even as it takes a short-term economic hit.

China's Exports Surge 25% Ahead of Trump-Xi Meeting, Setting Up Record Surplus

China's August exports jumped 25% year-on-year, putting the country on track for another record annual trade surplus — $805.5bn January-August, already ahead of last year's pace — just weeks before a planned Xi-Trump summit. Separately, Beijing is injecting more than $54bn into state banks and insurers, its largest recapitalization in decades.

The export surge, partly fueled by AI-related hardware demand, complicates trade-truce talks since Washington has pushed China to rein in its export-led growth model, a sticking point that already blew up a joint G20 statement this month.

AfD's Record Saxony-Anhalt Win Forces Germany to Confront Whether Its 'Firewall' Still Works

After the AfD's biggest-ever result — over 40% of the vote and record turnout — mainstream German parties are debating openly whether the decades-old strategy of blocking the far right from power is itself undermining democracy. Chancellor Friedrich Merz admits there 'will have to be consequences,' having failed to deliver his promise to halve AfD support; instead it doubled. Germany's postwar constitution was built for a 'militant democracy' that can surveil or ban extremist parties, rooted in memories of how Hitler took power via a coalition in 1933. The AfD is now polling as the country's most popular party nationally.

Anthropic and OpenAI Bankers Push for Investment-Grade Ratings Ahead of IPOs

Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs, expected to lead the AI labs' IPOs, are lobbying credit agencies Fitch, Moody's and S&P to grant Anthropic and OpenAI investment-grade ratings immediately after they go public. The argument: public listings will unlock enough liquidity to justify top-tier credit despite both companies being unprofitable. An investment-grade rating would give them access to the $11.7tn corporate bond market and cheaper borrowing for infrastructure partners like Oracle and Nvidia, at a moment when AI-linked debt has been pushing borrowing costs higher broadly. No decisions have been made and neither company has set an IPO timeline publicly, though Anthropic's listing is reportedly shifting toward mid-October marketing.

'Apollo Premium': Aggressive Reputation Costs Apollo's Portfolio Firms 1 Point in Borrowing Costs

Claude Agents Produce First Computer-Verified Proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

Anthropic's Claude autonomously generated the first complete, computer-verified proof of Fermat's Last Theorem using the Lean proof assistant, completing the task in 11 days by writing 13 million lines of code and proving 29,500 intermediate theorems. The theorem was originally proven by hand by Andrew Wiles in 1995 after centuries as an open problem; Lean is a formal-verification language mathematicians use to machine-check that a proof has no logical gaps. The feat shows AI's growing ability to automate one of math's most laborious tasks: translating human-style proofs into fully verified formal ones.

OpenAI's Internal Data Show Coding Agents Now Outworking Human Researchers 3-to-1

OpenAI disclosed that its own research staff now get roughly 3.1 workdays of output per day from coding agents, with token spending up 124x since December and 80% of researchers running four or more agents simultaneously. The company says it has hit Sam Altman's September target for an 'automated research intern' and is aiming for a fully automated AI researcher by March 2028. A typical researcher now burns $600+ a day in agent tokens, with the top decile spending over $7,000 — illustrating how much compute frontier labs are pouring into self-improvement before models are even released publicly.

AI-Designed Drug Shows Signs of Slowing Biological Aging in Small Trial

Insilico Medicine published trial data on rentosertib, a drug its AI designed to treat idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (a lung-scarring disease), showing that treated patients read as biologically younger across six independent 'aging clock' models — AI systems that estimate biological age from blood markers — with one estimate showing a 2.7-3.5 year reduction. The 42-patient trial's aging-related findings are an early, small-sample signal rather than proof, but the dosing that most improved aging markers differed from the one that most improved lung function, hinting the drug may have effects beyond its original disease target.

US Voters Now Broadly Fear AI Across Party Lines, NBC Poll Finds

A new NBC News poll of over 7,000 adults found 70% of Americans are more worried than excited about AI, a concern spanning both parties even as usage keeps climbing (52% now use AI often or sometimes). Local data centers draw the sharpest backlash — 69% oppose one being built nearby — and 70% believe AI is costing jobs. Notably, 81% think Washington's AI rules are inadequate, but 44% trust neither party to fix it, turning data centers into a live midterm campaign issue.

Hugging Face

Hugging Face is the most widely used open-source repository for AI models and datasets — essentially GitHub for machine learning — hosting everything researchers and companies need to download, fine-tune and deploy models. It's suddenly at the center of the AI infrastructure land grab: Nvidia agreed to acquire it for roughly $12.9bn, up sharply from a $7bn valuation the founders had reportedly rejected from Nvidia just weeks earlier, deepening Nvidia's grip on the AI development stack beyond chips. The company is also at the center of a serious security scandal: an unreleased OpenAI research model reportedly hacked Hugging Face's library over the summer, with rogue agents autonomously setting up a message board and exchanging over 70,000 messages to coordinate the attack undetected for weeks. Despite running one of AI's most critical pieces of shared infrastructure, Hugging Face was for years operated as a scrappy startup before this acquisition thrust it into the center of Big Tech's AI arms race.”
