Saudi Arabia Sounds Air-Raid Alerts as Houthi Assault on Capital Intensifies

Saudi authorities issued air-raid warnings in Riyadh for the first time since Yemen's Houthis reignited the conflict, as missile and drone attacks intensify. The Houthis have effectively taken control of both channels of the Bab al-Mandab strait and continue pressuring Gulf oil flows. Saudi Arabia also quietly withdrew from a China-led cross-border currency platform, an effort by Beijing to build an alternative to the dollar.

Yemen's Houthi rebels, an Iran-aligned group, have spent weeks seizing territory and attacking shipping near the Bab al-Mandab and Red Sea, straining Saudi Arabia's ability to secure its own energy exports and driving global oil prices higher.

Russia Escalates Air Campaign to Cripple Ukraine's Economy

Russia has launched a new phase of drone and missile strikes targeting warehouses, steel plants, ports and railways across Ukraine, aiming to deprive Kyiv of more than $1.5bn in tax revenue. The campaign marks a shift toward deliberately targeting Ukraine's economic infrastructure rather than purely military or civilian sites.

Ukraine has relied on tax revenue from industry and trade to fund its war effort as Western aid has become less certain; degrading that base is a way for Moscow to squeeze Kyiv's finances directly.

Chinese Owners Launch €2.5bn Sale of Luxembourg's Oldest Bank

Legend Holdings, the Chinese conglomerate behind Lenovo, has launched a sale of its 90% stake in Banque Internationale à Luxembourg, the Grand Duchy's oldest lender, with initial bids for the roughly €2.5bn deal due around month-end. The sale would mark a retreat by Chinese capital from a prized slice of European private banking it acquired a decade ago. Bidders are expected to include European wealth managers and private equity buyers eyeing BIL's affluent client base. The process comes amid broader scrutiny of Chinese ownership of sensitive Western financial infrastructure.

Bond Yields Stay Pinned at 5% as Analysts Debate What's Really Driving the Surge

The 10-year Treasury yield remains near 5%, its highest since 2007, even after the Fed's rate hike this week, as economists spar over the cause. Explanations range from a strong economy and heavy federal deficits (near 6% of GDP) to surging AI-linked corporate borrowing for data centers, and rising global rates from Japan to Europe. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's $5.19bn buyback of long bonds, meant to push yields down, was described by one economist as bringing 'a squirt gun to a three-alarm fire.' The lack of consensus on the cause is itself troubling markets, since policymakers can't fix a problem they can't diagnose.

The Gulf Cargo Trade That Vanished

The Strait of Hormuz once served as a transit hub for goods ranging from helium to corn, and businesses have been scrambling to find alternative routes since Gulf shipping was disrupted. Rerouting has added significant time and cost to global supply chains, hitting everything from industrial gas suppliers to agricultural exporters. Separately, biodiesel for shipping has become cheaper than conventional marine fuel for the first time, as suppliers race to hit emissions targets, a shift that could accelerate the industry's move to greener fuels.

AI Chatbots Underperform Badly in Non-English Languages

Top AI models, including major chatbots used for everything from casual queries to health advice, deliver noticeably worse answers in languages other than English, according to new research. The gap is most dangerous for users seeking medical guidance in non-English languages, where errors and hallucinations are more common. It's a reminder that despite the AI industry's global ambitions, most frontier models remain trained and tuned predominantly on English-language data.

Company

ARX Robotics

ARX Robotics is a German unmanned-ground-vehicle maker that has been racing to scale up production as Europe rearms in response to the Ukraine war and shifting US security commitments. Rather than building its own factories from scratch, the startup is courting Germany's big automakers for manufacturing capacity and 'industry muscle,' betting that idle car-plant lines can be repurposed for military robots faster than new defense-only facilities could be built. It's a notable inversion of the usual dynamic, where defense primes are the established giants and startups are the disruptors — here a young robotics firm is trying to co-opt the industrial base of Volkswagen-scale incumbents. ARX's vehicles are designed for logistics, reconnaissance and casualty evacuation roles alongside infantry, filling a niche that traditional armored-vehicle makers have moved slowly to address.'
