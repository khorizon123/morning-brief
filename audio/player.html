Global Bond Sell-Off Deepens as Japan's Yield Hits 3%

Japan's 10-year government bond yield hit 3% for the first time since 1996, and UK borrowing costs rose to their highest since 2008, as investors bet central banks will raise rates to fight inflation from higher energy prices following the US-Iran flare-up. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, meeting Japan's finance minister and BOJ governor at a G20 summit, signaled he expects the BOJ to raise rates soon, sending the yen higher. Eurozone inflation also rose to 3.3% in August, reinforcing bets the ECB will hike from 2.5% at its September 10 meeting.

Rising government bond yields raise borrowing costs for households, companies and governments worldwide, and are often a signal that inflation expectations are climbing.

AI & Tech

Runway's Solaris Previews a No-Code, Video-Rendered Internet

Runway unveiled Solaris, an 'Interface World Model' that renders entire websites and apps as live-generated video — every frame drawn in real time as a user clicks or drags, with no underlying code. It pairs Runway's Gen-4.5 video model with an LLM that interprets each interaction and prompts the next frame; demos included a virtual shirt try-on and an interactive combustion simulation. In Runway's own study, testers preferred Solaris over pages coded by Claude Opus 5 in 71% of matchups, though the system still struggles with text legibility and long-session drift. It's an early glimpse of interfaces that may not need traditional code at all.

Anthropic's Claude Agents Outperform Human Experts at AI Safety Research

Anthropic published research showing teams of Claude agents ran AI safety experiments autonomously, fixing 10 types of model misbehavior — including sycophancy, deception, and reward hacking — with results averaging over 4x better than human experts given the same tasks. Claude averaged an 85% deception fix rate versus 20% for six veteran human researchers, and a weaker Sonnet 5 model safety-trained a pre-release Opus 4.8 build using 15,000x less data than Anthropic's usual process. It's an early concrete sign of AI beginning to automate its own safety research, raising new questions about trusting non-human systems with that work.

Imperial College AI Flags Heart Disease From a Routine ECG in Under Two Seconds

Researchers at Imperial College London built a model that reads a standard ECG in under two seconds, catching heart failure and valve disease that doctors often miss without a lengthy ultrasound queue. Trained on 10.6 million ECGs and tested on 65,000 patients, it caught heart failure 81% of the time and valve disease 90% of the time. A 590-patient trial is starting at six hospitals, with hopes of folding the tool into routine NHS use within two years.
