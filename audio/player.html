Treasury Yields Hit Highest Since 2007 as Oil Tops $109 on Saudi Pipeline Shutdown

The 10-year Treasury yield broke above 5% for the first time since the financial crisis, as Brent crude climbed past $109 a barrel after Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline and Gulf states postponed talks with Iran over the Strait of Hormuz. Foreign investors are now buying more US stocks than Treasuries for the first time this century outside crisis periods, a sign confidence in government debt is eroding even as equities keep rallying on AI-driven profits.

Rising yields raise borrowing costs across the economy — mortgages, corporate loans, government debt service — and a durable break above 5% would mark a genuine regime shift after decades of falling rates.

Saudi Arabia in 'Strategic Shock' as Houthis Consolidate Grip on Bab al-Mandab

Days after Yemen's Houthis captured Mokha and seized Perim Island, giving them effective control of both channels of the Bab al-Mandab strait, Saudi Arabia is weighing whether it can mount a coalition to fight back — a war it lost decisively before — or accept a humiliating diplomacy track, with the US declining to get involved. Some ocean carriers, meanwhile, are cautiously returning containership services to the Suez route despite the unresolved risk, betting the Houthis will keep their word not to target non-Saudi shipping.

The Houthis are an Iran-backed Yemeni militia that has disrupted Red Sea shipping since 2023; their new control of the strait connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden gives them outsized leverage over global oil and container trade even as Iran's own war with the US/Gulf states continues.

Supreme Court Blocks Trump's Mail-Voting Restrictions Ahead of Midterms

The Supreme Court rejected the Trump administration's attempt to overhaul mail-in voting rules ahead of November's midterms, upholding a lower court order and dealing a blow to a plan the White House pushed on unproven fraud claims. Justices Alito and Thomas dissented; elections officials across party lines welcomed the ruling since many states had already printed and mailed ballots.

Trump has pushed to restrict mail voting since March, arguing without evidence that it enables fraud; Democrats currently hold a polling edge in the midterms per a new NYT/Siena survey.

Markets & Deals

Michael Dell's Family Office Beats KKR in $7.7bn Baldwin Group Buyout, Signaling AI-Native PE Wave

Michael Dell's DFO Management and upstart holding company Sequence Holdings outbid private equity giants including KKR to take insurance broker The Baldwin Group private for $7.7bn — at roughly a 20-times multiple traditional PE firms considered too rich. The deal underscores a broader shift: firms like General Catalyst, Long Lake and Sequence, often staffed by ex-Apollo and Oaktree associates, are building AI-driven roll-up strategies aimed at extracting efficiencies from real-economy businesses (insurance, travel, wealth management) the way financial engineering once did. Skeptics note no one has yet shown concrete evidence the AI efficiencies are real, but the entrance of tech-native buyers gives traditional PE a genuine new competitor for capital deployment.

Vy Capital's Secretive $50bn Bet on Musk Pays Off Big

Vy Capital, a nearly invisible venture firm run by Alexander Tamas and John Hering, has quietly become one of Elon Musk's largest backers, with its SpaceX stake alone now worth roughly $40bn — up from a $15bn-valuation entry a decade ago. The firm's total assets have grown to about $50bn (from $27bn at end of last year), a 41% gross IRR since 2014, built through early stakes in SpaceX, BoringCo, Neuralink and a $700mn commitment to Musk's 2022 Twitter buyout. Vy is now closing to new outside capital, but the partners tell investors they see SpaceX potentially reaching a $10tn valuation within five to seven years.

India's Central Bank Forces Tata Sons Toward What Could Be Nation's Biggest-Ever IPO

India's Reserve Bank rejected Tata Sons' latest bid to escape rules requiring it to list, telling the sprawling conglomerate's holding company it must comply 'immediately.' Tata Sons — which controls roughly two dozen listed businesses including Tata Consultancy Services, Jaguar Land Rover and Air India, but has kept its holding company private under opaque charitable trusts — could be worth over $120bn, putting a listing in contention for India's largest-ever IPO. The forced flotation would also reshuffle power inside the Tata dynasty: chairman Noel Tata has resisted a listing, and tension over the issue contributed to chair N Chandrasekaran's abrupt resignation last month.

Trump and Beijing Both Reject Amodei's Call for an AI Slowdown

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's weekend essay urging frontier labs to deliberately 'pace' AI development got rejected by both governments capable of enforcing it. Trump called AI-doom warnings a 'hoax' on Truth Social and said the only guardrail needed is a 'STRONG AND SMART' president; China's foreign ministry separately dismissed the plan as 'fear-mongering' aimed at containing China. Microsoft published its own 38-page 'Humanist AI' code of conduct the same day, arguing models must remain subordinate to humans, avoid mimicking consciousness, and accept being switched off — a notable break given Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman's past clashes with Anthropic over AI 'welfare' talk. Meanwhile Sam Altman confirmed OpenAI won't IPO in 2026, citing safety concerns as an 'ill-advised' moment to go public, and SoftBank borrowed a further $11.9bn from roughly 20 banks to keep funding OpenAI toward Masayoshi Son's ~$65bn commitment.

Apple Ships Long-Delayed Siri AI Overhaul as English-Only Beta

Apple released iOS 27 with the rebuilt Siri AI, co-developed with Google's Gemini models, that can read on-screen content, search messages/email/photos for context, and take actions inside apps like WhatsApp and Audible. It launches with daily usage caps, English-only support, and exclusions for the EU and China, with paid tiers likely later. New CEO John Ternus has argued the iPhone remains AI's best device — a claim that looks more like an absence of competition than genuine Siri superiority, given the beta's limits.

Company

Sequence Holdings

Sequence Holdings, which brands itself 'the holding company of the next century,' just outbid established private equity giants like KKR to take insurance broker The Baldwin Group private for $7.7bn, backstopped by Michael Dell's family office. The firm is part of a new wave of tech-native investment vehicles — alongside General Catalyst and Long Lake — staffed largely by former mid-level associates from traditional PE shops like Apollo and Oaktree who are betting AI can generate efficiencies in real-economy sectors (insurance, travel, wealth management) that financial engineering alone couldn't. Its Baldwin bid came in at roughly a 20-times cash-flow multiple, several turns richer than the ~17-times PE firms have recently paid for insurance brokers, a bet that's raised eyebrows even among AI enthusiasts. Surprising fact: despite the audacious 'next century' branding and eye-watering price tag, skeptics note nobody — including Sequence itself — has yet publicly demonstrated concrete evidence that its AI-driven efficiencies actually exist at scale.
