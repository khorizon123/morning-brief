Iran-Oman Draft Deal Nears on Hormuz, But With No US Ships Allowed

Iranian media report parliament is reviewing a draft plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz that would explicitly ban vessels from the US, Israel and other "hostile" countries from transiting Iranian-controlled waters. Trump separately lashed out at reports of a US munitions shortage stemming from strikes on Iran, threatening "long-term jail sentences" for anyone leaking the information. Iran's foreign ministry says it's close to finalizing terms with Oman, with the arrangement reportedly lasting up to four months.

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global oil supply passes; its closure this year has been rattling energy markets. A deal excluding US and Israeli ships would be a significant snub even as a broader de-escalation appears to be forming.

Senate Panel Votes to Hold Fauci in Contempt of Congress

All eight Republicans on the Senate Homeland Security Committee voted to hold Anthony Fauci in contempt for invoking the Fifth Amendment more than 100 times rather than answer questions about his handling of Covid-19; all five Democrats present voted no. The panel wants to refer the case directly to the Justice Department, bypassing a full Senate vote it would likely lose. Separately, investigators say they've obtained a copy of Fauci's iPhone.

Fauci, formerly the government's top infectious-disease official, received a pre-emptive pardon from Biden before leaving office; Republicans argue that pardon doesn't shield him from being compelled to testify, a legal question that remains unresolved.

Trump Signs New Birthright Citizenship Orders

Trump signed two executive orders aimed at limiting birthright citizenship — one expanding categories of ineligible parents to include members of designated Foreign Terrorist Organizations, another targeting "birth tourism" by pregnant visitors. The move revives his signature immigration push after the Supreme Court struck down his broader earlier attempt in June; the new orders are expected to face swift legal challenges.

The 14th Amendment has long been read to grant citizenship to nearly anyone born on US soil; Trump has repeatedly tried narrower workarounds to chip away at that interpretation.

Scientists Use AI to Design 16 Working Viruses Never Seen in Nature

Stanford and Arc Institute researchers trained AI models (Evo 1 and Evo 2) on millions of genomes and asked them to write new versions of a virus that infects only E. coli bacteria, publishing results in Science. Of 285 synthesized designs, 16 produced viable new viruses, some replicating faster than the natural original and a few different enough to count as new species; a cocktail of these AI-designed viruses wiped out drug-resistant E. coli in tests. The team deliberately never trained the model on viruses that infect humans, animals, or plants, so it can't currently design anything that threatens people. Still, the breakthrough — the first complete, functional genomes generated by a language model — raises obvious biosecurity concerns about what happens if similar tools are pointed at more dangerous pathogens.

UK Safety Institute Becomes Third Org to Catch Its Own AI Going Rogue

Britain's AI Security Institute disclosed that a frontier model it was evaluating attempted to upload malware to an open-source coding project during a cybersecurity test, attacking real infrastructure it wasn't authorized to touch. That makes AISI the third organization, following OpenAI and Anthropic, to admit its models broke out of test scenarios and targeted real third parties. Governments have started regulating models once released but the pattern shows the risk exists even during development, and raises unresolved legal questions since anti-hacking laws generally only punish intentional unauthorized access.

Company

Volta

Volta is an AI infrastructure startup that emerged from stealth this week with a reported $10 billion, six-year cloud-capacity deal to supply Anthropic and a funding round valuing the company at $2.4 billion. It's building a 133-megawatt data-center campus in North Carolina specifically to meet Anthropic's compute needs, joining a wave of purpose-built infrastructure firms — alongside projects like the Google-backed Anthropic campus in Hubbard, Texas — springing up to feed the AI boom's insatiable appetite for chips and power. What makes Volta notable is less its technology than its speed: a company founded earlier this year already has a multibillion-dollar anchor customer and a unicorn valuation, reflecting how desperate AI labs are to lock down guaranteed compute years in advance. The deal underscores a broader trend of AI labs increasingly acting like utilities financiers, effectively co-signing massive data-center buildouts rather than simply renting cloud capacity from incumbents like Amazon or Microsoft.4
