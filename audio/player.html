US-Canada Trade War Ignites as Talks Collapse and 50% Tariffs Hit

Talks between Washington and Ottawa collapsed late Friday night, and the US imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods after a three-day pause expired. Canada's PM Mark Carney declared the country is now "at war" with the US over trade, blaming last-minute US demands including an unexplained exemption for medium and heavy trucks from auto-tariff relief. Carney says Canada will impose dollar-for-dollar countertariffs on US steel, dairy and appliances starting September 8. Trump countered that Canada wants "the benefits of being a State, without being one."

Canada and the US have an almost $900bn-a-year trading relationship, and this marks the most serious breakdown in that relationship in decades, with both sides now escalating rather than de-escalating.

Bessent Unveils 'Economic D-Day' Sanctions Push on Iran

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is announcing sweeping new sanctions targeting Iran's oil trade, calling it "the single greatest financial offensive ever marshaled against an adversary." Without naming China directly, Bessent singled out Iran's oil-buying "enablers" — China imported an average 1.4mn barrels a day of Iranian crude last year. The move follows a collapse of ceasefire talks and continued disruption to Strait of Hormuz shipping, which has fallen to 4-6mn barrels a day from a pre-war 20mn.

The US and Iran have been at war for six months following the collapse of ceasefire talks; Iran's economy and currency have already been battered by a US naval blockade, and Beijing is Tehran's largest oil customer, making sanctions on Chinese buyers a major flashpoint.

Wheat Crisis Deepens as Drone Strikes and Drought Hit World's Breadbaskets

Russia and Ukraine have stepped up attacks on each other's Black Sea grain terminals and ships, with Novorossiysk's grain terminals suspended after a drone strike and Odesa hit by Russian bombing. Meanwhile France, Germany, Australia and the US Great Plains are all facing sharply lower wheat yields due to heat, drought and high fertilizer costs stemming from the Strait of Hormuz closure. Wheat prices hit a three-year high in July, and countries like Bangladesh have been pressured into overpaying for US wheat to avoid Trump tariffs.

Russia, Ukraine, Canada, the US and the EU dominate global wheat exports, and their customers are mostly low-income countries that no longer grow much of their own food, making the system highly vulnerable to simultaneous shocks.

Panama Canal Slashes Transit Slots as El Niño Threatens Drought Repeat

The Panama Canal Authority will limit daily transit slots starting September 4 — cutting Neopanamax slots to nine and Panamax slots to 25, tightening further to 23 by September 15 — as water levels drop ahead of a forecast strong El Niño. Watershed inflows are already running 44% below the historical average for the current hydrological year. Some container lines have already added surcharges and adjusted rates in anticipation of delays. The Authority is also postponing planned draft increases originally set for late August.

Mystery Model 'Ox Alpha' Sparks Guessing Game Over Its Origins

An anonymous AI model called Ox Alpha appeared for free on OpenRouter with a 1M-token context window, multimodal input and frontier-level coding claims, immediately sending developers scrambling to identify its creator. It initially scored 80% on a DeepSWE coding subset, though fuller testing put it closer to 63% — still competitive with OpenAI's Fable 5 at a fraction of the tokens. Early forensic clues, including its Chinese zodiac-style naming, point to Zhipu AI's GLM-5.3 Flash or GLM-6, though Microsoft's MAI family is also floated as a candidate. It's the fourth unlabeled model drop on OpenRouter in six months, and all previous ones traced back to Chinese labs.

Data Centers Become a Political Liability for Big Tech

71% of Americans oppose a data center being built in their local area, according to a March Gallup poll — more than the 53% who'd oppose a nuclear plant nearby. Politicians who once championed data centers, like Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro, are now imposing strict guardrails on new construction. In response, OpenAI, Microsoft and Amazon are hosting community listening sessions, offering job guarantees and funding local investments; OpenAI even held an open house with a taco bar near its planned Georgia data center. The backlash is increasingly colliding with the industry's massive buildout ambitions.

Lady Gaga's Outer Bio Keeps Human Skin Alive for AI-Driven Drug Discovery

Outer Bio, co-founded by Lady Gaga and fiancé Michael Polansky, emerged from stealth with Yuna, a platform that keeps real human skin alive on a 3D-printed scaffold for four weeks (versus about one week normally), feeding the resulting data into an AI that proposes new skincare compounds. Polansky says the loop has cut the time to find a new candidate from about 18 months to every six weeks, with six leads currently active. A bioRxiv preprint shows Yuna reproducing known results, including reversing a psoriasis-like state with a JAK inhibitor. It's still a partial model of skin, lacking blood flow or immune cells.

Cerebras Systems

Cerebras builds giant wafer-scale AI chips designed to run inference far faster than Nvidia GPUs, and it just unveiled its newest CS-4 chip alongside an "Ultrafast" service tier for OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol model running at up to 750 tokens per second. It's suddenly in the spotlight for an unrelated reason: OpenAI quietly exercised every vested warrant it held in the company for just $100 days before that launch, turning a nominal stake into holdings now worth roughly $2.3 billion. Cerebras has struggled for years to go public, but its speed advantage has made it the go-to partner for AI labs wanting faster response times rather than more raw compute. The company's wafer-scale chips are physically the size of a dinner plate — orders of magnitude larger than a typical GPU die.”
