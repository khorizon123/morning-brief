Trump Envoys Head to Moscow With Ukraine Peace Proposal as Air Strikes Pause

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Moscow carrying a Ukraine peace proposal, with Russia and Ukraine agreeing to a temporary pause in air strikes for the duration of the visit. Former MI6 chief John Sawers said at the FT Weekend Festival that an enduring peace remains hard to conceive of while Vladimir Putin stays in power, suggesting real talks may not begin until next year.

Washington has been trying to broker an end to the Russia-Ukraine war for years; this trip follows a Kyiv drone strike on Ukraine's own security headquarters and months of on-off diplomacy.

US Strikes Three Iranian Oil Tankers as Hormuz Fighting Escalates

American forces struck three Iranian oil tankers in retaliation for attacks on US warships, the latest escalation in the six-month conflict over the Strait of Hormuz. Separately, air-cargo giant SATS said US energy firms are rushing machinery and materials into the region to repair war-damaged infrastructure, boosting its business.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most critical oil chokepoints; sustained fighting there has kept energy markets on edge and pushed diesel and gasoline prices to record highs in recent months.

Trump's Approval Rating Hits All-Time Low Weeks Before Midterms

An FT poll shows Donald Trump's approval rating has fallen to its lowest point since he took office, driven largely by voter anxiety over the economy. The slump comes weeks before November's midterm elections and adds pressure on Republicans already contending with record diesel prices and an ongoing war footing over Iran.

Presidential approval ratings heading into midterms are historically a strong predictor of how badly the incumbent party performs down-ballot.

Junk-Debt Spreads Widen to Post-'Liberation Day' Highs as Treasury Sell-Off Bites

Spreads on the riskiest US corporate junk debt have climbed to their highest level since last year's tariff-driven bond-market meltdown, as the broader Treasury sell-off pushes borrowing costs higher across the credit spectrum. Wall Street analysts and investors are growing more openly critical of the Trump administration, particularly after a Treasury intervention aimed at calming the bond market. Some strategists are now questioning whether traditional fiscal stimulus can even work when debt itself is the underlying problem — a debate captured in FT commentary asking whether Keynesianism is dead. The pressure is compounding for weaker borrowers just as global bond yields have already surged over the past week on inflation and rate-hike bets.
