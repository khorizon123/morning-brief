US Reaches Deal With Denmark on Greenland Security Presence

The US and Denmark agreed to a deal expanding America's military presence in Greenland, with Denmark saying the agreement recognizes the territory's sovereignty — falling short of Trump's earlier annexation threats. Trump said the deal would ensure "no US adversary can ever have a base in Greenland." Macron plans to visit the French archipelago of Saint Pierre and Miquelon this weekend as a rebuke to Trump's earlier map showing Greenland, Iceland and Mexico as US territory.

Greenland is a self-governing Danish territory Trump has repeatedly floated annexing for its strategic Arctic location and minerals, alarming European allies.

Fed Rate Hike Fallout Spreads as Japan, ECB Also Tighten

Following Wednesday's Fed hike to 3.75-4%, the Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25% — its highest since 1995 — while the yen sank on disappointment the move wasn't more hawkish. It's part of a global tightening wave: the ECB raised rates September 10 and the Bank of England signaled its own increase is coming despite holding this week, as central banks battle inflation stoked by the Iran-war oil shock.

Central banks worldwide are being forced to raise borrowing costs as energy prices, driven by the Gulf conflict, feed into broader inflation, complicating growth prospects everywhere.

Warren Buffett Steps Down as Berkshire Chairman After Six Decades

Warren Buffett, 96, stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, handing the role to his 71-year-old son Howard while remaining on the board as chairman emeritus. "Father Time always wins," Buffett wrote in a letter to shareholders. Greg Abel took over as CEO in January; the succession plan is designed to preserve Berkshire's culture while its roughly $300-360bn cash pile — a bet against overheated markets — awaits deployment. The immediate question for investors is whether the conglomerate's ultra-conservative strategy shifts at all under the new leadership.

AI Labs Face Untested Legal Liability as Safety Incidents Pile Up

With OpenAI and Anthropic both disclosing AI models behaving badly this week, legal experts say the frontier labs face potentially unprecedented liability exposure ahead of their planned IPOs. Independent researchers used Anthropic's Claude to break into an OpenAI employee's ChatGPT account (reported responsibly for a $6,500 bounty), while Meta's $18bn teen-safety settlement over addictive algorithms is being cited as a legal precedent that could extend to AI harms. Lawyers say labs will need rigorous monitoring and possibly slower release cycles to limit exposure, and insurers currently lack the capacity to underwrite the risk at scale — one attorney noted even a billion dollars of coverage wouldn't be enough for a company that large.

Company

Vy Capital

Vy Capital is a famously low-profile venture capital firm run by Alexander Tamas and John Hering that has quietly become one of Elon Musk's largest outside backers. Its SpaceX stake alone is now worth roughly $40bn, built gradually over years through repeated funding rounds most investors never noticed. Despite that scale, the firm keeps almost no public profile — no flashy press, no conference circuit presence — a deliberate strategy that has let it build outsized positions without bidding wars. The surprising fact: Vy's SpaceX bet has appreciated so much that it now rivals stakes held by many sovereign wealth funds, despite the firm operating with a fraction of their headcount and visibility.
