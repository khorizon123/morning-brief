Bessent's Bond Buyback Puts Treasury on Collision Course With Fed

Wall Street investors are warning that Scott Bessent's move to double Treasury purchases of long-term debt is pulling against the Fed's inflation fight, raising the stakes for new Fed chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech Friday. Billionaire investor Stanley Druckenmiller — a mentor to both Bessent and Warsh — wrote a scathing WSJ op-ed calling the move an attempt to defy market fundamentals, warning "governments defending prices against fundamentals always lose." The intervention has had only a muted effect on yields so far.

The Fed sets interest rates to control inflation, while the Treasury manages government borrowing; when Treasury buys back its own long-term bonds to hold down yields, it can undercut the Fed's tightening efforts and blur the line between fiscal and monetary policy.

Canada Confirms $20bn Retaliatory Tariffs, Trump Renaming Lake Ontario

Canada detailed its retaliation against the US tariffs imposed last week, doubling tariffs on steel and aluminum to 50% and adding levies of 15-50% on goods like furniture, clothing, dairy and machinery starting September 8. Trump escalated the rhetoric by musing about renaming Lake Ontario "Lake America," while JD Vance criticized Canada's military — drawing rebukes from Republicans and former US military leaders who called the attacks 'embarrassing.'

This follows the collapse of US-Canada trade talks and the imposition of 50% US tariffs on ~$20bn of Canadian goods, with Prime Minister Mark Carney declaring Canada now effectively at war over trade with its largest partner.

Iran and Oman Edge Toward Hormuz Shipping Deal as Oil Slides

Iran and Oman are again discussing a joint temporary navigational corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, sending Brent crude down to $86 a barrel from $88. China, which buys over 90% of Iranian oil, called new US sanctions on Tehran illegal and said its trade with Iran "should not be interfered with or disrupted."

The US and Iran have been in a six-month conflict partly over Gulf shipping lanes; the Strait of Hormuz is a chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global oil flows, so any easing of tensions there moves markets.

CIA director John Ratcliffe spent about four hours in Moscow for talks whose purpose remains unclear, arriving via a US military-transport flight routed through Latvia; the Kremlin initially denied knowledge of the visit. SpaceX Commits $100bn to New Louisiana Launch Site

SpaceX will spend at least $100bn building a second Starship launch complex, dubbed "Starbase, Louisiana," west of New Orleans, aiming to support thousands of annual rocket flights and create at least 3,000 permanent jobs. Construction begins next year, and the company is also adding two launch pads in Florida. Shares in the Musk-led company rose on the news, though local residents are split — some cheer the jobs, others worry about environmental costs to an area known for duck hunting and fishing.

Nvidia Backs $6bn Poolside Licensing Deal to Build Open-Weight Rival

Nvidia is licensing AI coding startup Poolside's technology for $6bn while investing $1bn directly at a $12bn valuation, absorbing over 100 Poolside engineers into its Nemotron team to build an American open-weight model to counter Chinese rivals like DeepSeek and Z.ai. The move could strain Nvidia's relationships with closed-model customers like OpenAI and Anthropic, who rely on Nvidia chips but now face Nvidia-backed competition. Separately, Samsung confirmed a record $78.88bn buyback and dividend plan to share AI-driven chip profits with shareholders, even as its shares slid on the announcement.

Emerald AI Raises $150m to Make Data Centers Grid-Friendly

Emerald AI, a two-year-old startup whose software helps data centers flex their power draw to ease strain on electrical grids, raised $150 million at a $1.05 billion valuation led by Energize Capital and DCVC, with Nvidia, Samsung Ventures and GE Vernova also participating. The company takes a cut when utilities capture extra revenue from more efficient grid use and charges data center operators for freed-up capacity. It's tested its software at five sites so far, with contracts in California and Virginia, positioning itself as an answer to growing local backlash against power-hungry data centers.

OpenAI's First In-House Chip 'Jalapeño' Beats Nvidia on Speed and Efficiency

OpenAI published benchmarks for Jalapeño, its custom inference chip built with Broadcom, showing it beats Nvidia's flagship systems by answering up to 3.6x faster while drawing less power (700 watts versus 1,200). OpenAI says it used its own Astra model and Codex to help design the chip in just nine months, though it won't sell Jalapeño externally and still relies on Nvidia for training. Two more chip generations are already in the works, joining a growing club of AI labs — including Google, Anthropic, Amazon and Microsoft — building their own silicon to cut costs and tighten hardware-software integration.

Mysterious 'Ox Alpha' Model Breaks OpenRouter Records

An anonymous AI model called Ox Alpha appeared for free on OpenRouter and OpenCode with a 1-million-token context window and frontier-level coding claims, processing 26 trillion tokens and drawing 327,000 unique users in its first four days — breaking OpenRouter's launch record. Its listing lacks any information on the maker, release date, or knowledge cutoff, sparking a guessing game among developers about its origins. The mystery underscores how quickly powerful new models can spread with zero attribution in today's open AI ecosystem.

OpenAI and Google Both Battling AI Talent Exodus, With Different Stakes

At least a dozen high-profile OpenAI employees have left this year, including its chief revenue, operating, marketing and product officers, just as the company gears up for a potential trillion-dollar IPO — a departure OpenAI partly enabled by letting staff cash out $6.6bn in shares last year. Google has seen its own shake-up, losing chief scientist Jeff Dean (after 27 years) along with Nobel laureate John Jumper and AI pioneer Noam Shazeer, prompting Google to promote Demis Hassabis and Koray Kavukcuoglu into bigger roles. Analysts say Google's deep bench makes its losses easier to absorb than OpenAI's.

Nvidia's Groq 3 LPX AI inference accelerator, an extension of its Vera Rubin platform, is now in full production, delivering what the company calls the fastest token-generation speeds ever recorded and a 4x boost in response times over the nearest rival platform. The chip targets response-sensitive agentic AI workloads, aiming to compress tasks that take hours into minutes — part of the broader race among chipmakers to own the inference layer as AI agents proliferate.

Startup Backed by Caltech Professor Bets on 'Physics AI' Over Text Prediction

Caltech professor Anima Anandkumar and engineer Benedikt Jenik launched Accelerated Understanding, training AI on "neural operators" that model how the physical world evolves through 3D space and time rather than predicting the next word. The pair turned down a 35% stake in Jeff Bezos-backed rival Prometheus (which has since raised $12bn) to build their own venture, and claim their model processed 5 trillion data points in a single run — roughly 5 million times what Google and Anthropic's top models handle. Early targets include chip materials, extreme-weather prediction and robotics.

Company

Kalshi

Kalshi is a federally-regulated prediction market that lets users trade contracts on real-world event outcomes, from elections to sports. It's currently fighting more than 20 US states that accuse it of hosting illegal sports gambling — while simultaneously expanding fast, striking brand partnerships with five MLB teams (the Red Sox, Dodgers, Braves, Giants and Padres) that include stadium signage and app promotions. The Dodgers deal is quirky enough to include naming rights to a stadium bar, the 'Gold Glove Bar.' Kalshi is also negotiating a league-wide MLB deal, even as rival Polymarket has already locked up official league gameplay data and its own team deals with the Yankees. The company argues its contracts fall under federal commodities law, not state gambling regulation — a legal theory now being tested state by state.
