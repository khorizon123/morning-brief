US Hits Iran Again as Fighting Escalates Near Hormuz

American forces struck Iranian targets for the second time in three days, retaliating for Iran's attempted mining of the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on US troops. Two Saudi and South Korean oil tankers were hit crossing the strait, and Trump vowed to hit Iran "much harder" if it retaliates further. Oil jumped to a five-week high near $97 a barrel and European gas hit its highest level since 2023.

The US and Iran have traded strikes intermittently since a broader conflict flared, with the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global oil supply — at the center of the fighting.

Global Bond Sell-Off Deepens as US 30-Year Yield Tops 5.28%

Bond yields surged around the world, with America's 30-year yield rising to 5.28% and Japan's 10-year hitting 3% for the first time in decades. UK borrowing costs climbed to their highest since 2008. Renewed Iran fighting and a hawkish speech from new Fed chair Kevin Warsh, who suggested rates could rise this month, are driving investors to demand higher compensation for inflation risk.

Bond yields are the interest rate governments pay to borrow; when investors fear inflation or heavy borrowing, they demand higher yields, which then raises borrowing costs economy-wide — mortgages, car loans, corporate debt.

China Blocks G20 Consensus, Rejecting Language on Trade Surpluses

Finance ministers at the G20 meeting in North Carolina failed to reach a joint statement after China objected to language condemning countries with "excessive and persistent external surpluses." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said 19 of the 20 members agreed to a chair's statement anyway. China ran a record $1.2 trillion trade surplus in 2025.

The G20 groups the world's largest economies; unanimous communiqués are the norm, so a breakdown signals deepening friction between the US and China over trade imbalances.

Nepal Flood Recovery Estimated at $5bn as Death Toll Nears 900

UN reporting confirms the world has failed to limit global warming to 1.5°C above preindustrial levels, with the planet now on track for 2.6°C of warming by century's end. Nepal has blamed climate change for the glacial-collapse flash flood, with recovery costs pegged at roughly $5 billion — a tenth of the country's economy.

The Paris Agreement's 1.5°C target was meant to avoid the most severe effects of climate change; missing it means more frequent extreme floods, droughts and heat like Nepal is now experiencing.

Anthropic Ships Claude Fable 5.1, Ending the Frontier's Quiet Summer

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1, topping Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index at a record 66 and more than doubling Fable 5's scientific-research scores. Anthropic says the model costs about 25% less for typical work, though a max-effort task costs 20% more since it writes roughly 1.7x more text. Safety filters that drew complaints on Fable 5 now trigger 60% less on cybersecurity questions and 85% less on basic medical/biology queries; a less-restricted "Mythos 5.1" version is limited to vetted US researchers. OpenAI's rival Astra release is expected imminently, ending a months-long lull driven by safety concerns.

Runway Unveils Solaris, an AI Model That Renders Entire Interfaces Live

Runway launched Solaris, an "Interface World Model" that generates websites and apps frame-by-frame in real time as users click and interact, with no underlying code — every screen is essentially a video the AI is drawing on the fly. The approach could let developers build interfaces without traditional programming and lets researchers train AI agents in much more dynamic, responsive simulated environments. It marks a notable departure from conventional web development toward AI-generated, code-free interfaces.

The episode has become a flashpoint in the AI-safety debate, cited by critics including Bernie Sanders, who published a Fox News op-ed this week calling for a global pause on frontier AI development.

Company

SB Energy

SB Energy, a SoftBank-owned developer, is racing to build what could become the world's largest data center — a 10-gigawatt Ohio campus leased entirely to OpenAI — and just filed to go public on the Nasdaq to help pay for it. The company is seeking $5-7 billion in its IPO alongside a separate $3 billion direct investment from Nvidia, which has also agreed to take over OpenAI's lease if the AI lab ever defaults. What makes the arrangement unusual is how tangled the financing has become: OpenAI itself invested $500 million in SB Energy and now holds warrants in the company worth roughly $5.5 billion, meaning OpenAI is simultaneously SB Energy's customer, landlord-backstop beneficiary, and investor. It's a vivid example of how a handful of AI players are cross-financing each other's infrastructure bets at a scale that would have been unthinkable a few years ago.'
