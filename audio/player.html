Skyfall AI Wants to Automate the Job of CEO

Sam Pasupalak, who sold his previous startup Maluuba to Microsoft for $160 million in 2017, is now building Skyfall AI, a startup training AI to run entire companies rather than just write code. The team, which first tested its system on the video game RollerCoaster Tycoon, is closing its first acquisition of a small SaaS company via marketplaces like acquire.com, spending under $1 million on experiments before targeting $10-100 million businesses next year. Company

Skyfall AI

Founded by Sam Pasupalak, who previously sold his AI startup Maluuba to Microsoft for $160 million in 2017, Skyfall AI is trying to automate not software engineering but the job of chief executive itself. The company is acquiring small SaaS businesses through marketplaces like acquire.com and TrustMRR to serve as live experiments, aiming to run them with minimal human involvement while an AI system sets strategy, manages go-to-market decisions, and reacts to sales performance. It first tested its approach in the video game RollerCoaster Tycoon before moving to real acquisitions this year, and plans to scale from sub-$1 million experiments to businesses worth $10-100 million next year. Pasupalak's stated rationale is stark: he argues that even the most hyped AI software engineers are 'so easy' compared to the harder problem of long-horizon, high-stakes decision-making under uncertainty. Surprisingly, he still thinks human relationships at work can't be automated away — the company plans to keep its target businesses to just one to three employees rather than zero.
