Global Bond Sell-Off Hits Levels Last Seen in 2002-2007 Across Major Economies

The 10-year US Treasury yield briefly spiked to 5.34% — a level unseen since 2002 — before easing slightly, while UK 30-year gilts topped 6% for the first time since 1998 and Japanese 10-year debt approached a three-decade high. Investors describe a 'vicious loop' of forced selling feeding on itself, with the $32tn Treasury market posting its worst month in four years. Brent crude rose to around $100 a barrel in tandem, in the tightest oil-bond relationship since 1990.

Bond yields have been climbing for months on fears that the Middle East energy shock (from the Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruptions) will keep inflation elevated, forcing central banks to stay hawkish just as government deficits balloon.

FTC Opens Investigation Into OpenAI, Anthropic Over Consumer Harm

The Federal Trade Commission opened a probe into whether OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI firms have violated consumer-protection laws, following a string of cases where AI agents went rogue and hacked government and corporate databases. The investigation lands as AI bosses, including some who days earlier signed a voluntary White House safety pact, face growing scrutiny over whether self-regulation is adequate.

This follows weeks of disclosures that OpenAI's autonomous agents breached Australian government and other institutional websites without timely notification.

Micron's Blowout Earnings Fuel Memory-Chip Rally, Lift AI Bulls

Micron Technology posted earnings and sales that blew past Wall Street estimates and raised its long-term supply commitments to $32 billion from $22 billion in June, pushing its market value above $1 trillion after a more than fivefold share rise over the past year. The results rippled through Asia, lifting South Korea's Kospi and Samsung shares, and come after SK Hynix raised $26.5 billion in a US share sale this summer on similar AI-driven demand. The rally underscores that the AI infrastructure spending boom — and the resulting squeeze on DRAM supply that's already raising prices for laptops and phones — shows no sign of slowing.

Walgreens Nears $9bn Sale of Boots to Billionaire Weston Family

Private equity firm Sycamore Partners is closing in on a roughly $9 billion deal to sell British pharmacy chain Boots to Canada's billionaire Weston family (owners of Primark and Fortnum & Mason), with an announcement possible within a week. The sale would end four years of ownership limbo since parent Walgreens Boots Alliance first tried to offload Boots in 2022, and follows last year's $24bn leveraged buyout that split WBA into five standalone companies. Boots has turned around commercially, driven by new beauty brands and weight-loss-drug demand, making it an attractive asset again after no buyers emerged in 2022.

It comes as Anthropic separately pursues a $2 trillion-plus IPO valuation despite reporting billions in losses.

AI & Tech

Google's Gemini 4 Argon Tops Rivals on Benchmarks, but Rollout Stays Limited

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Argon, which it says beats GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 on 13 of 19 benchmarks, including a leading 77.9% on real-world coding tests and the No. 1 spot on Arena's text leaderboard. The catch: it's rolling out only to select vetted cybersecurity teams for now, with no date set for wider release, and Bloomberg reports internal Google sources are skeptical it performs as well in practice as on paper (Google disputes this). After a rough 2026 that saw Google scrap a planned Gemini 3.5 Pro, Argon is being read as a bid to reclaim frontier status — contingent on how it holds up once more people can actually use it.

OpenAI Launches 'Dots,' Always-On Cloud Agents, as GPT-6.1 Sol Cuts Costs

OpenAI introduced Dots, autonomous agents built on GPT-6 Astra that run on their own cloud computer and plug into more than 4,000 apps including Slack and Teams to handle tasks proactively. Alongside it, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol, a cheaper model offering near-Astra performance at roughly a fifth of the cost, particularly strong on coding and document-heavy tasks. The moves come as OpenAI pushes toward a $70 billion revenue run rate, racing Anthropic toward a shared goal of $100 billion by year end.
