Trump Insists on 'Total Control' of Hormuz as Traffic Data Says Otherwise

Trump claimed the US has 'total control' of the Strait of Hormuz even as ship-tracking data showed only 14 vessels crossed the waterway yesterday, versus more than 130 a day before the war. Iran maintains the strait won't reopen until Washington meets its conditions, including reparations and lifting the naval blockade. Pakistan, mediating between the two sides, says it remains 'optimistic' about a deal.

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil transits; a prolonged closure has kept global energy markets on edge for weeks despite Brent crude barely moving.

Colombia Earthquake Death Toll Passes 250, Coffee Exports Disrupted

The death toll from Monday's 7.4-magnitude earthquake in western Colombia has climbed past 250 people, with new president Abelardo de la Espriella facing his first crisis just three days into office. The quake has also disrupted operations at the port handling most of the country's coffee bean shipments, a blow to the world's third-biggest coffee industry.

Colombia is a major global coffee exporter, and the earthquake struck cities including Cali, Pereira and Manizales, flattening buildings and displacing residents.

Ukraine Halts Black Sea Tanker Strikes After Vance Request, But Novorossiysk Attack Shows Escalation

Ukraine paused an intense campaign of drone strikes on tankers loading at Russia's Novorossiysk port after US Vice President JD Vance personally asked Zelenskyy to stop, worried the attacks were destabilizing oil markets. Yet a fresh attack on Novorossiysk this week — one of Ukraine's most sophisticated hits yet on Russia's Black Sea coast — shows the underlying escalation continuing, crimping operations at the Caspian Pipeline Consortium that carries almost 2% of world oil.

The Black Sea is a critical export corridor for both Russian and Ukrainian grain and oil; escalating strikes on tankers and ports risk pushing up global food and energy prices, particularly hurting import-dependent countries in Africa.

Bob Iger and Josh Kushner Buy Lakers for Record $12.5bn

Just over a year after buying the Los Angeles Lakers at a record $10bn valuation, financier Mark Walter agreed to sell the team to former Disney CEO Bob Iger and Josh Kushner (brother of Jared Kushner) at a $12.5bn valuation — the highest price ever paid for a sports franchise. Nvidia Assembles $500bn 'Chip Financing' Deal With Wall Street Titans

Nvidia is teaming with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to build more than $500bn in 'compute financing platforms' — vehicles that buy Nvidia chips and lease them to AI companies, then securitize those leases for pension funds, insurers and retail investors, much like aircraft-lease or auto-loan markets. Jensen Huang calls it a new asset class backed by chips; critics like Apollo's own Marc Rowan have previously warned about levering the retirement system to Nvidia. The bet hinges on chips holding their value over the long haul — a risk given how fast GPU generations depreciate.

Apollo Buys $2.6bn Stake in New York Yankees

Apollo Sports Capital agreed to a $2.6bn investment in Yankee Global Enterprises, the parent company of the New York Yankees, valued by Forbes at $8.5bn — the most valuable MLB franchise. The Steinbrenner family keeps operating control, with Hal Steinbrenner staying on as managing general partner while Apollo gets a board seat. The capital, a mix of debt and equity, will let the team refinance debt and fund new investments. It follows a wave of private-equity sports deals — 52 in 2026 already, on pace to top last year's 71 — as investors increasingly see franchises as a portfolio diversifier.

US Inflation Cools Slightly to 3.4%, Buying the Fed More Time

US consumer prices rose 3.4% year-over-year in July, down from 3.5% in June, with core CPI (ex-food and energy) at 2.5%, in line with expectations. The data eases pressure on the Fed to consider rate hikes, though officials remain sensitive to signs the economy isn't behaving as forecast — poorer Americans are increasingly struggling, one top Fed official told the FT. US electricity prices, meanwhile, rose 4.2% year-over-year amid the hottest month on record.

Company

Lovable

Lovable is a Stockholm-based 'vibecoding' startup that lets nontechnical users build and run full software applications through AI, without writing code. It just raised $400 million at a $13.3 billion valuation — more than double its December mark — with annual revenue surging to nearly $600 million. Rather than targeting engineers, Lovable focuses on entrepreneurs and small businesses who use its platform to run entire operations, connecting to payment systems, analytics and messaging tools. Nvidia and Adidas are now among its fastest-growing corporate customers, a surprising foothold for a tool built for non-coders. The company has also had to weather a security scare: in April a user on X found they could access other customers' source code and chat histories, a lapse critics called a cautionary tale for the vibecoding boom broadly.
