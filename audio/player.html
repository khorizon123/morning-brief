Iran and Oman Finalize Draft Deal to Reopen Strait of Hormuz

Iran's foreign ministry says it has reached agreement with Oman on a proposed shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz that would give Tehran oversight of ships entering the Persian Gulf without letting it levy tolls or fees. Trump administration officials say a deal to reopen the waterway is close, though Iran's deputy foreign minister cautioned it wouldn't necessarily open unless the US meets unspecified conditions. Oil prices steadied on the news, with Brent trading around $79-80 a barrel.

The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint, and fighting between the US, Israel and Iran has periodically threatened to close it, rattling energy markets for months.

Ukraine's Air War Intensifies as Kyiv Runs Low on Interceptors

Russia launched another blistering missile attack on Kyiv, killing at least 17 people and wounding 44 as neither side lets up on strikes targeting critical infrastructure. Ukrainian officials confirmed not a single one of the 24 ballistic missiles or four hypersonic missiles fired at the capital was intercepted, an indication that Ukraine's Patriot batteries are running critically low on interceptors. Ukraine has meanwhile stepped up drone strikes on Russian oil facilities, ports and logistics hubs, turning the war into an increasingly aerial contest.

Russia has escalated missile and drone attacks on Ukrainian cities throughout the summer even as international attention has shifted toward the Iran conflict and Gaza; Western supplies of Patriot interceptors have failed to keep pace with the barrage.

El-Sayed's Michigan Win Fuels a National Political Fight Over Data Centers

Abdul El-Sayed's razor-thin win in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary is being read as a template for progressives: he campaigned partly on a moratorium against data centers, in a state where OpenAI is building a large, unpopular facility. The same night, Kansas Democrats nominated a gubernatorial candidate who has made opposing data centers central to her platform. More than half of Americans say they'd support a national ban on the facilities, and nearly four in five worry about data centers raising their electricity bills.

Data centers needed to train and run AI models consume enormous amounts of electricity, and their costs are increasingly being passed to ordinary ratepayers, turning what was a wonky infrastructure debate into a live political issue.

Citadel's Fire-Sale Rescue of Situational Awareness Pays Off Big

Ken Griffin's Citadel made billions of dollars in a single week after buying the bulk of the public stock book of Situational Awareness, the AI-focused hedge fund run by 24-year-old Leopold Aschenbrenner, in a forced fire sale. Citadel's flagship fund returned about 6% in July — its best month since 2022 — while its equities fund gained 14.2%, pushing its 2026 return to 27%. Aschenbrenner's fund had notched 400%+ gains in the first half of the year on leveraged AI bets before a broad AI stock selloff forced it to dump its positions in under 24 hours. It's the latest example of Citadel swooping into distressed situations, echoing its purchases of Amaranth's and Sowood's books in 2006 and 2007.

Company

Volta

Volta is an AI infrastructure startup that emerged from stealth this week with a reported $10 billion, six-year cloud-capacity deal with Anthropic and a funding round valuing the company at $2.4 billion. Its flagship project is a planned 133-megawatt data center in Norway, developed with Bitdeer and powered by Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chip systems. Volta is part of a wave of specialized compute providers — alongside firms like Nexus Data Centers — that have sprung up specifically to lock in long-term capacity for AI labs racing to secure power and chips outside the traditional hyperscaler ecosystem. The surprising fact: the company went from stealth to a $10 billion customer commitment essentially overnight, underscoring just how desperate frontier labs are for guaranteed compute regardless of counterparty track record.
