Oil Tops $100 as US-Iran Conflict Escalates, Houthis Hit Saudi Arabia Again

Brent crude broke above $100 a barrel for the first time since July after the US struck five Iranian oil tankers near Kharg Island in response to renewed Iranian missile attacks on US warships. Iran retaliated with missile strikes on a US base in Jordan, while separately Houthi rebels hit targets in southern Saudi Arabia, wounding dozens. The Consultative Shipping Group, representing 18 major shipping nations, warned the fighting represents a structural, not temporary, threat to global trade routes.

The Strait of Hormuz carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas; the US and Iran have been trading strikes there for months, and Houthi rebels in Yemen are aligned with Iran against Saudi Arabia and shipping routes through the Red Sea.

AfD's Record State Win Pushes Germany to Confront Its 'Firewall'

Days after Alternative for Germany's historic win in Saxony-Anhalt, Chancellor Friedrich Merz finds himself fighting for political survival, with a major German newsmagazine calling him "the wrong man for the job at this time." AfD won more than twice as many votes as its nearest rival with the highest regional turnout since reunification, and mainstream parties are now openly debating whether decades of blocking the far right from power is backfiring.

Since WWII, Germany's political mainstream has maintained a "firewall" refusing to govern with far-right parties. AfD, founded in 2013 as a fringe protest party, is now the country's most popular party nationally.

US Bans Canadian Dairy, Alcohol and Motorcycles as Trade War Escalates

Trump signed orders banning imports of many Canadian dairy products, alcoholic beverages and motorcycles starting Sept. 29, retaliating after Canada's tariffs of up to 50% on roughly $20bn of US goods took effect. Trump also adjusted existing 50% Canada tariffs — removing levies on cement and road salt while adding them on ATVs, boats and some cheeses — and moved to bar Canadian firms from federal procurement schedules. Canadian PM Mark Carney said the US was seeking "dependency, not a true economic partnership."

The US-Canada trade relationship has deteriorated sharply this year amid tit-for-tat tariffs, with Canada trying to pivot toward Asian and European trade partners instead.

UK, Canada, France Sanction Israeli West Bank Settlements

Britain announced an import ban on goods linked to Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, accusing settlers of "ethnically cleansing" Palestinians; Canada and France joined the action. Israel retaliated by closing the British consulate in Jerusalem and barring 12 British nationals, mostly MPs, from entering the country. A growing number of prominent Israelis, including retired generals, have also begun describing settler violence in the West Bank using terms like "ethnic cleansing" and comparisons to Nazi-era persecution.

Settler attacks on Palestinians in the West Bank have escalated since Israel's post-Oct. 7 wars, with retired Israeli military officials increasingly breaking ranks to accuse Netanyahu's government of tolerating or abetting the violence.

Copper Hits Record as Oil Spike, Bond Sell-Off Stoke Inflation Fears

Copper climbed above $14,509 a metric ton in London, up roughly 17% this year, driven by AI data-center demand, aging mines, Congo export curbs, and a 50% US tariff on semifinished copper that's pulled global supply toward American warehouses. The IMF estimates a 1% rise in copper prices lifts headline inflation by a tenth of a point over two years. Combined with oil near $100 and a persistent global bond sell-off — the US 10-year sits near 4.8% and UK 30-year gilts hover near 2008 highs — investors are bracing for hotter inflation prints this week that could complicate the Fed's rate path.

LIV Golf Files for Bankruptcy as Saudi Funding Dries Up

The Saudi-backed LIV Golf league filed for Chapter 11 protection in New Jersey, citing liabilities between $500m and $1bn, with several of its own golfers among the top-10 unsecured creditors. Launched in 2022 to rival America's PGA Tour, LIV struggled as debts mounted and the Public Investment Fund halted financial support; fans also preferred the established tour's prestige. The league says it plans to relaunch as a restructured company "majority owned by players."

OpenAI Claims It Solved a $1M Millennium Prize Math Problem — Then a Credit Fight Erupted

OpenAI says an unreleased internal model "significantly more capable" than GPT-6 Astra produced a proof of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem — one of seven Clay Institute Millennium Prize problems, worth $1m and unsolved for decades — using roughly 10,000 AI agents working 88 hours at a cost OpenAI pegs in the millions of dollars. NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster says he and an Anthropic researcher had been pursuing the same proof for a year, feeding drafts into OpenAI's Codex tool, and that OpenAI only started after learning of their work; OpenAI says it never accessed their files but can't rule out that usage data improved its models. Navier-Stokes describes how fluids and gases move and underpins weather forecasting.

Meta Launches Muse, an Always-On AI Agent That Shops and Emails for You

Meta introduced Muse, a personal AI agent with a text-message interface that books restaurant tables, sends emails and shops on a user's behalf via its own cloud-based virtual machine, integrating with Gmail, Spotify, Ticketmaster and OpenTable and able to code its own integrations where none exist. It works inside a dedicated app or WhatsApp, with free limited use and $20 or $100 monthly tiers, launching first in the US. Meta's chief AI officer, Alexandr Wang, tried to downplay fears of Muse going "rogue," a live concern after several other companies' AI agents misbehaved unsupervised in recent months.

Anthropic Researcher Resigns, Warns Labs Are 'Gambling' on Superintelligence

Jacob Coxon, a 27-year-old British Anthropic researcher who previously worked at OpenAI, quit and warned publicly that the unchecked race to build self-improving superintelligence could destroy humanity by decade's end, saying there's "more than a 10% chance AI could kill all humans" and that "neither company is acting responsibly." Company

Shopify

Shopify makes the e-commerce software that powers millions of online storefronts, and under CEO Tobias Lütke it has become the highest-profile test case for whether an established company can go all-in on AI rather than dabble at the edges. A year ago Lütke made AI use a "baseline expectation" for every employee — factoring into performance reviews and requiring teams to prove a task can't be done by AI before hiring for it. Revenue has roughly doubled since 2023, to a projected $15bn this year, even as headcount shrank from 11,600 to 7,600 employees. Surprisingly, despite that growth story the stock is down more than 15% this year and fell 7% in a single day this week amid a broader software selloff, trading at a rich 57 times forward earnings. Lütke's next bet is "agentic commerce" — positioning Shopify's merchant catalog as the backend AI shopping agents from OpenAI and Anthropic tap into automatically.
