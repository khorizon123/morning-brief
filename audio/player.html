US and Iran Trade Fire for First Time in a Month, Reigniting Strait of Hormuz Fears

American forces struck two rocket launchers on Iran's Larak Island near the Strait of Hormuz after intelligence suggested Iranian soldiers were preparing to fire mines into the strait. Trump claimed Iran's main oil export terminal at Kharg Island was 'blown to smithereens,' though that's unverified. Iran retaliated with missiles at American military targets in Jordan, and Brent crude jumped to $90 a barrel.

The US and Iran have been in an on-again, off-again conflict for months over Iran's nuclear program and its threats to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for roughly a fifth of the world's oil.

Nepal-Tibet Flood Toll Nears 800 as Rescue Effort Intensifies

Nepal's death toll from last week's glacial-collapse flash flood has climbed past 800, with more than 3,000 still missing. Rescue teams from China and India are using specialist tunnel equipment to search hydropower sites damaged in the disaster, while authorities have begun mass burials of unidentified bodies. The government estimates rebuilding will cost $4-5bn, roughly a tenth of Nepal's economy.

A high-altitude glacier collapse and possible thawing permafrost triggered the flood on the Nepal-Tibet border last Wednesday, sending a wall of water and debris more than 80km down the valley with no rainfall involved.

Trump Administration's Murky Venezuela Oil Deal Takes Shape

Details emerged of how the Trump administration structured its plan to secure a fifth of Venezuela's oil reserves: after US energy companies balked at investing quickly, Washington made the US government itself an investor, taking a 35% passive stake in North American Blue Energy Partners, which holds Venezuelan rights. A controversial Venezuelan insider, Alejandro Betancourt, is emerging as a key partner courting outside investors alongside the US government.

The Trump administration ousted Venezuela's government and has been negotiating a long-term lease over the country's roughly 65bn barrels of oil reserves, one of the largest deposits in the world.

Niger Coup Attempt Fails, Soldiers Detained

Niger's government regained control of a military base attacked by mutinying soldiers who briefly seized the state TV station and struck the presidential palace and airport. The disgruntled soldiers who launched the attack have since been detained in the capital, Niamey.

Niger has seen repeated coup activity in recent years amid regional instability across the Sahel, with juntas in several neighboring countries having pushed out Western influence in favor of Russian ties.

El Niño of the Century Threatens Global Supply Chains

The 2026-27 El Niño is shaping up as one of the strongest on record, tightening the Panama Canal just as shipping is squeezed from the Strait of Hormuz to the Rhine — one tanker operator reportedly paid $5.3m for priority passage. Chile's Antofagasta cut copper output forecasts after storms shut its Los Pelambres mine, tightening an already stretched copper market. Economists expect the pattern to bring slower growth and higher inflation, especially across emerging Asia.

Meta Settles Teen-Safety Suit for Up to $18bn Mid-Trial

Meta abruptly settled with 48 states mid-trial over claims it hooked children on Instagram and Facebook, agreeing to annual payments over a decade and product changes including two-hour daily limits for under-18 users and blocked overnight scrolling. Unusually, Meta says it will only pay the full $18bn if TikTok and YouTube agree to matching time limits and their own $5.3bn payments. Florida rejected the deal and plans to proceed to trial, calling the payout 'peanuts'; New Mexico had already won its case separately.

OpenAI Pulls Its Models From Cursor After SpaceX Acquisition

OpenAI will remove its AI models from coding platform Cursor by November 12, citing Elon Musk's history of violating contracts after SpaceX acquired the once-neutral coding editor last month. OpenAI pointed to Musk killing a $2M-a-year data deal after buying Twitter, and to xAI training on OpenAI's outputs. Musk responded that he 'couldn't care less,' while Cursor's CEO said OpenAI's models make up only about 5% of the platform's traffic and pushed for a fix. The spat drags a previously neutral developer tool into the broader Altman-Musk feud.

Court Rules Pentagon Illegally Blacklisted Anthropic

A federal judge ruled the Pentagon broke the law by labeling Anthropic a 'supply chain risk' — a designation usually reserved for adversaries — finding it was retaliation for the company's pushback on military AI use rather than a genuine security concern. The label had forced Anthropic's Mythos and Fable models offline for nearly a month and made it the first US firm ever hit with the designation. Judge Rita Lin said national security isn't a 'blank check' to punish critics, though the label technically survives pending a separate appeal, and the Pentagon says its Claude wind-down will finish by September 30.

Cognition Labs

Cognition, maker of the autonomous coding agent Devin, is reportedly raising new funding at a $46bn valuation — nearly double what it was worth just three months ago, with backers including 8VC, Founders Fund and B Capital. Devin bills itself as an AI 'software engineer' that can independently plan, write, test and debug code rather than just autocomplete it. The valuation surge reflects a broader frenzy around AI coding tools, a category now drawing direct competition from Nvidia's Poolside investment and OpenAI's own coding push. Remarkably, Cognition was founded only in 2023, meaning its valuation has ballooned nearly 50-fold in about two years.
