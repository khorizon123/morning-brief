Bessent's 'Economic D-Day' on Iran Turns Out to Be a Warning Shot

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled sweeping new sanctions dubbed 'Operation Economic Outcast,' targeting more than 60 entities, vessels and individuals that help Iran move money, sell oil and procure technology — but named no countries and set no deadlines, despite billing the announcement as an economic 'D-Day.' The rial breached 2 million to the dollar, a record low, and Iran's president Masoud Pezeshkian separately said Tehran should end the war 'from strength' while security officials threatened to fine or seize 46 vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Iran is betting Washington's political constraints — high fuel prices, looming midterms — mean it can wait out the pressure rather than negotiate.

The US and Iran have been in an undeclared war for roughly six months, with Washington blockading Iranian oil exports in hopes of forcing Tehran back to nuclear talks. China buys over 80% of Iran's oil exports and has so far resisted US pressure to cut ties.

Trump Threatens 50% Tariffs on Canadian Autos From January as Trade War Deepens

Trump said the US will raise tariffs on Canadian cars, auto parts and steel to 50% starting January 1st, escalating beyond the 50% tariffs already imposed on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods after talks collapsed last week. Canada's PM Mark Carney has declared the country 'at war' with the US and plans retaliatory levies on American steel, dairy, appliances and electronics from September 8th. A rift has reportedly opened inside the Trump administration, with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick pushing back on a framework negotiated by trade rep Jamieson Greer.

Canada and the US have been locked in an escalating tariff fight for weeks after a three-day negotiating truce expired; the dispute threatens the USMCA trade pact and could raise prices on goods like cars, jackets and plywood for American consumers.

Supreme Court Lets Trump's Mail-In Voting Restrictions Proceed for Now

The Supreme Court issued an emergency order allowing Trump to move forward with a March executive order directing Homeland Security to compile state-by-state citizen lists and instructing the Postal Service to potentially refuse delivering mail-in ballots in noncompliant states. The three liberal justices dissented, with Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson warning the ruling 'needlessly injects chaos' into the midterms. The court said the decision wasn't final on the order's legality, meaning the fight will likely return to the justices before Election Day.

Mail-in voting has become a partisan flashpoint in the US; the ruling could reshape voting-access rules with just over two months left before this year's midterm elections.

SEC Subpoenas Wall Street Banks Over Collapsed AI Hedge Fund

The SEC has reportedly subpoenaed several Wall Street banks over the implosion of Situational Awareness, an AI-focused hedge fund run by 24-year-old Leopold Aschenbrenner with no prior trading experience. The fund borrowed heavily to bet on AI stocks but was forced into a fire-sale in July's tech sell-off before striking a rescue deal with Citadel. Regulators want details on the fund's trading activity and its conversations with the banks that supplied leverage.

The episode highlights how much leverage has piled into AI-related trades, raising questions about risk controls at the banks financing them.

Gold Nears $4,700, Bitcoin Tops $80,000 as 'Debasement Trade' Resumes

Gold rallied to nearly $4,700 per troy ounce, its highest since May, while bitcoin climbed back above $80,000 as investors piled into anti-fiat 'debasement trades.' The move follows the Treasury's decision last week to double its buyback of long-dated bonds in an attempt to cap yields, which has instead reinforced fears about runaway US debt (now above $40trn) and a widening 6%-of-GDP deficit. The 30-year Treasury yield has whipsawed between roughly 5.18% and 5.34% over the past week despite Bessent's intervention.

SpaceX and Nvidia Team Up to Put Data Centers in Orbit by Late 2027

SpaceX announced it will build its Starmind orbital data centers around Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack — a cluster of 72 chips acting as one computer, each up to 25x more powerful than an older H100 — with Elon Musk targeting first orbital racks by Q4 2027. Musk says the space-adapted version is simpler, lighter and cheaper than terrestrial hardware, tuned for radiation and heat, and plans to run everything from Grok to SpaceX's satellite fleet on Nvidia chips exclusively. Orbital compute today costs more than 4x as much as ground-based compute, a gap Musk claims will close within a few years. The push comes as ground-based data centers face mounting local opposition in the US.

Thomson Reuters Builds Its Own AI Model to Cut Reliance on Anthropic

Thomson Reuters unveiled its first homegrown AI model, built by retooling Alibaba's open-source Qwen and training it on decades of proprietary legal and news content from Westlaw and Reuters. The two-year effort cost $40m total, with the latest training run alone costing $450k — cheap relative to the company's prior API spending on rented models. Thomson claims the model benchmarks ahead of Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-5.5 in some areas, though testing has only been internal so far, and an open-weights version will be released to researchers covering under 10% of its content library.

Chinese State Hackers Doubling Attack Volume Using DeepSeek

China-linked hacking groups are carrying out more than twice as many attacks as before after integrating open-source models like DeepSeek into their toolkits, according to Taiwanese research firm TeamT5. Hackers reportedly favor DeepSeek for its low cost and minimal safety guardrails, using it to write intrusion code, breach a Taiwanese firm's email system and map targets via roughly 1,000 addresses; pricier, better-guarded models like Kimi K3 haven't shown up in tracked attacks. The finding echoes a UK AI Security Institute warning that AI cyber capabilities are advancing rapidly — suggesting cheap, loosely-guarded open models may be a harder security problem than frontier ones.

Company

Outer Bio

Co-founded by Lady Gaga and her fiancé Michael Polansky, Outer Bio emerged from stealth with a platform called Yuna that keeps real, donated human skin alive on a 3D-printed scaffold for up to four weeks — about four times longer than standard lab tissue survives. The company trains AI models on the biological responses of this living tissue to accelerate drug discovery, aiming to shortcut the slow, expensive process of testing compounds in more traditional settings. It's a strange pairing of pop-culture fame and hardcore biotech infrastructure, and the model has drawn early attention precisely because it sidesteps synthetic skin substitutes that biologists have long complained don't behave like the real thing. The surprising fact: the skin tissue used is literally kept alive and metabolically active outside the body for weeks at a time, essentially functioning as a tiny biological lab in itself.
