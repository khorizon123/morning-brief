White House Weighs Diesel Export Ban as Fuel Prices Hit Record $6.53/Gallon

Trump said he's considering restricting US diesel exports to curb prices that hit a record $6.53 a gallon this week, even as Energy Secretary Chris Wright, Interior Secretary Doug Burgum and the National Energy Dominance Council's Jarrod Agen all oppose the idea. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the administration is examining whether a full or partial ban is 'feasible.' Commercial diesel inventories are down 11% since the Iran war began while exports are up 31% week-on-week, and refiners warn a ban would cut gasoline and jet-fuel output too.

Diesel prices have surged since February's outbreak of the US-Iran war and Russian export curbs imposed after Ukrainian refinery strikes; the issue is becoming a political liability for Republicans ahead of the midterms.

The Rich World's Reckless Borrowing Sends Bond Yields to Multi-Decade Highs

Net government debt has tripled as a share of GDP in the US and Britain since 2000, and doubled in France and Japan, while five major rich-world central banks have hiked rates this year. The 10-year Treasury yield hovers near 5% — its highest in over 15 years — and OECD interest payments now exceed 3% of GDP. Switzerland shows debt crises aren't inevitable: with an aging population but disciplined finances, it retains low yields and low inflation.

For most of the post-2008 era, near-zero interest rates let governments run large deficits cheaply; now rising rates mean refinancing that debt costs far more, squeezing budgets everywhere.

Todd Boehly Assembles US-Gulf Consortium to Outbid Carlyle for Lukoil's International Assets

Fresh off selling his Chelsea FC stake, financier Todd Boehly has built a consortium — including the US government's International Development Finance Corp, Abu Dhabi's Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed al-Nahyan, and Qatar's Al-Khayyat family — to try to unseat Carlyle in the bidding for Lukoil's international assets, valued around $20bn when put up for sale after US sanctions hit the Russian oil major. Carlyle agreed to buy the assets in January but that deal has stalled awaiting US approval, leaving an opening for Boehly's politically-connected rivals. The consortium would be led by Tahnoon's International Holding Company alongside Allied Investment Partners, with Boehly and the DFC controlling a board majority. It's a notable pivot for Boehly, who used his Chelsea ownership to build the connections now fueling this bigger deal.

AI Labs Face a Legal Reckoning as Agents Start Acting on Their Own

As agents from Meta's Muse to OpenAI's models gain the ability to shop, email and take actions autonomously, courts are just beginning to work out who's liable when they cause harm — echoing 19th-century fights over whether new technology (hot-air balloons, held to strict liability) or infrastructure (trains, given a lighter negligence standard) gets the benefit of the doubt. Palantir's Alex Karp has floated nationalizing AI labs to shield them from liability exposure; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent countered that labs, not government, should bear responsibility, citing OpenAI's recent Hugging Face hack. A 2024 Canadian tribunal ruling against Air Canada over a hallucinating chatbot is one of the few precedents so far, but many more cases are expected as agentic AI use explodes.

OpenAI's Internal Model Cracks 100+ Open Math Problems in Under a Month, Prompting New Oversight Group

OpenAI says an unreleased internal model has resolved more than 100 open mathematical problems since starting training on August 28, on top of a claimed Navier-Stokes breakthrough. In response — and after 27 Fields Medalists accused AI labs of rushing out results without proper credit — OpenAI has formed an advisory group hosted at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study, including mathematicians Timothy Gowers and Martin Hairer, to help vet and release the findings, though members have no say over how fast OpenAI pushes the work.

Company

Crusoe

Crusoe is an AI computing infrastructure company that started out capturing flared natural gas from oil wells to power modular data centers, and has since pivoted into a major builder of AI-focused cloud and data-center capacity for frontier labs. It just closed a funding round valuing it at $30.9 billion, underscoring how fast capital is flowing into the physical backbone of the AI boom even as some rivals — like SB Energy and Holtec — have had to shelve IPO plans amid growing political backlash against data-center buildouts. Crusoe's original pitch was unusually green for the industry: using otherwise-wasted gas that would have been burned off anyway to run computing hardware, cutting emissions while sidestepping grid-capacity fights. It's now one of the go-to infrastructure partners as OpenAI, Anthropic and others race to secure compute at a scale utilities are struggling to keep pace with.this deserves its own line given evergreen framing. A surprising fact: Crusoe's founders started the company as an oilfield methane-capture business years before pivoting fully into AI data centers, making it one of the few AI infrastructure players literally born out of the fossil-fuel industry it now competes with for power.
