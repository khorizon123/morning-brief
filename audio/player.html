Houthis Seize Key Red Sea Islands as Saudi Arabia Shuts Vital Pipeline

Iran-backed Houthi rebels captured Yemen's Perim Island and cemented control over both channels of the Bab al-Mandeb strait after fierce fighting, while Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline — which bypasses Hormuz entirely — as a "precautionary measure" following drone attacks launched from Iraq. Brent crude traded above $104-105 a barrel, with analysts warning of $120 oil, and the IEA now says the Strait of Hormuz won't reopen to shipping this year.

Iran and Yemen's Houthi movement have spent months squeezing global oil-shipping chokepoints amid the wider US-Iran conflict; the East-West pipeline was one of the last major crude routes not exposed to the Hormuz fighting.

US Inflation Stays Hot at 3.4%, Fed All But Certain to Hike Rates

August consumer prices rose 3.4% year-on-year, matching July's pace, while diesel hit a record $6 a gallon. Interest-rate futures now imply an 85% chance the Fed raises rates a quarter point next week — up from 70% before the data — which would be the first hike in three years under new chair Kevin Warsh, despite Trump's public opposition.

The Fed has held rates steady for years; a hike now would mark a sharp reversal driven by stubborn inflation, oil-driven price pressure, and a bond market that's already been selling off hard.

LIV Golf's Chapter 11 Filing Reveals $5.5bn Burned, Player Equity Scramble Underway

LIV Golf formally filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Trenton, New Jersey, court filings showing the Saudi-backed league spent $5.5bn in debt and equity since 2021 while ending up with just $15mn to its name. The Public Investment Fund has put in a $50mn bankruptcy loan to fund a possible "LIV 2.0" restructuring backed by $300mn from BC Partners, but that plan must firm up within a month or the league faces wind-down. Star golfers including Jon Rahm, Bryson DeChambeau and Cameron Smith are among unsecured creditors owed millions on contracts LIV is trying to terminate, with a settlement offer of equity in the new venture whether they want to keep playing or not.

Bond Sell-Off Deepens as Bessent's $6bn Buyback Fails to Calm Markets, Fed Rate Hike Looms

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's $6bn debt buyback operation — his largest intervention yet — proved insufficient to stem the surge in borrowing costs, with investors warning the bond market's "fever" hasn't broken. Ten-year Treasury yields remain elevated as diesel and oil prices at multi-year highs feed inflation fears just as the Fed weighs its first rate hike in three years. Junk-debt spreads and global government bond yields (Japan, UK, US) all sit near multi-year or decade highs, squeezing corporate borrowers and highlighting how tightly linked energy shocks, inflation, and debt markets have become.

Meta's $18bn Teen-Safety Settlement Includes Time Limits, Overnight App Blocks

Meta reached a historic $18bn settlement with 48 states over social media's harm to teenagers, avoiding a landmark trial where CEO Mark Zuckerberg was set to testify. Under the deal, Instagram and Facebook will default to a two-hour daily time limit for teens, block the apps overnight, and disable push alerts during school hours (8am–3pm) — though parents can turn the restrictions off and messaging is exempt. Notably, $5.3bn of the payout is contingent on rivals TikTok and YouTube adopting similar restrictions, since state AGs and Meta both view the harms as industry-wide. Florida refused to join, calling the payout "peanuts," and is instead betting on a solo trial after New Mexico won nearly $1bn from Meta earlier this year.

L Catterton Backs Hyrox Buyout at €600mn Valuation

Consumer-focused private equity firm L Catterton, part-owned by LVMH, has backed a founder-led buyout of Hyrox, the fitness-racing competition that mixes running with gym-based obstacles, valuing the German-founded business at roughly €600mn. Silicon Valley firm WNDR, founded by ex-DreamWorks boss Jeffrey Katzenberg, also joined the deal. Hyrox expects 2mn participants across its 2026-27 season, up from just 80,000 in 2022-23, and organizers say demand still outstrips supply, with some events forced onto a ballot system. L Catterton was an early Peloton investor and also holds stakes in Hydrow, Solidcore and Equinox.

OpenAI released the Agents API in public beta, giving developers direct access to the managed agent harness that powers its Codex coding tool — handling context, tools, subagents, persistent execution, files and code environments for agents that run over long stretches. Anthropic disclosed in a new threat-intelligence report that a Yemen-based cell tied to the Iran-backed Houthis attempted to use its Claude models to develop guidance, control and navigation software for ballistic missiles, though the resulting missile test apparently failed. 24 Fields Medalists Object to OpenAI's Handling of Its Math 'Breakthrough' Claim

Twenty-four Fields Medal-winning mathematicians have written a public letter objecting to how OpenAI has publicized an internal model's disputed proof of the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize questions. Mathematicians say the claim, generated using 2.7 million agent messages over 88 hours, has not been properly verified and worry the framing overstates what was actually accomplished. It's the latest flashpoint in a running credit dispute over whether AI-generated proofs deserve the same trust as peer-reviewed mathematics.

Company

Oura

Oura makes smart rings that track sleep, heart rate, and fertility signals, and it's now heading toward a public listing at a valuation of roughly $16bn after sales grew 74% in the nine months to June. CEO Tom Hale insists Oura is "not a consumer-electronics company" but a health-care one, envisioning a future where the ring flags problems and routes users to telemedicine (it already partners with Counsel Health) or in-person doctors armed with their own data. Its fertility-tracking features have built an unusually devoted following among women, with Hale describing being stopped in airports by users who credit the ring with helping them conceive. Surprising fact: about 5 million smart rings are expected to ship this year industry-wide, and IDC estimates most of them will be Ouras.
