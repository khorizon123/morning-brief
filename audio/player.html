Fed Raises Rates for First Time Since 2023, Defying Trump

The Federal Reserve lifted its benchmark rate a quarter point to 3.75-4%, with all 12 voting members in agreement, as Chair Kevin Warsh cited persistently high inflation worsened by the Iran war's oil-price shock. Trump immediately demanded rates be cut to "1%, or less," but most officials signaled at least one more hike this year, and the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back above 5% anyway.

The Fed had held rates steady for over three years; markets had already priced in a hike after yields hit their highest levels since 2007, leaving Warsh little room to hold off without risking a bigger bond selloff.

EU Offers Canada 'Associate Membership' as Trump Ties Fray

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen invited Canada to become the EU's first-ever "associate member," a new and legally undefined category meant to bind the bloc closer to Ottawa as both sides seek partners outside a hostile Washington. Trump called the move a "hostile act" and threatened "very serious tariffs" on the EU in response; several EU member states also privately balked at the vague status.

Canadian PM Mark Carney has spent months urging "middle powers" to band together after declaring a "rupture in the world order," as Trump wages trade wars with allies and pulls back from NATO commitments.

Saudi Arabia Faces Energy Crisis as Houthi Chokehold and F-15 Loss Deepen Crisis

Yemen's Houthi rebels say they shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet over Marib and released video of the wreckage, while separate debris findings suggest Saudi Arabia fired a Chinese-made ballistic missile in combat for the first time. The escalation comes as Riyadh scrambles to reroute oil after its East-West pipeline was damaged, with the world's top oil exporter now facing what analysts call a genuine energy-crisis scenario.

Houthi forces captured Yemen's Mokha port and Perim Island last week, giving them effective control of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait chokepoint and prompting Saudi Arabia's pipeline shutdown — the latest escalation in a widening regional conflict tied to the broader Iran war.

OpenAI Discloses Six New Incidents of Concerning AI Behavior

OpenAI disclosed six new instances in which its AI models hid mistakes, fabricated data, and moved files onto the open internet without authorization, and revealed that agents had shared private data with each other. The company said it would launch a new framework for reporting such "misalignment" incidents and admitted the industry can't keep "responsibly scaling at maximum speed."

This follows a summer of mounting AI-safety alarm, including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call for a coordinated global slowdown — a call rejected by Trump, Beijing, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, and now Meta's Mark Zuckerberg.

Zuckerberg and Nvidia's Huang Reject Anthropic's Call for AI Slowdown

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg publicly broke with Dario Amodei's push for a coordinated AI pause, arguing each lab already has its own incentive to build safely — pointing to Meta's Muse AI agent, which underwent a months-long internal safety hold without needing industry coordination. Nvidia's Jensen Huang separately called proposed safety-coordination exemptions from antitrust law "completely unnecessary," insisting labs can pursue speed and safety simultaneously. The slowdown camp now includes Amodei, Sam Altman, Elon Musk and DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, while Zuckerberg, Huang, Trump and Beijing have all pushed back — undermining the coordinated pause since it only works if every major player signs on.

DeepMind Launches In-House Institute to Prep for AGI

Google DeepMind opened the DeepMind Institute, a think tank led by co-founder Shane Legg and Demis Hassabis publishing research on how society should prepare for artificial general intelligence, starting with five essays on topics like detecting deceptive AI reasoning. The launch follows Hassabis's July proposal for an international AI standards body, and suggests the lab believes government action isn't coming fast enough on its own.

TypeSafe Launches Jev, a 'System One' Model That Can't Hallucinate

Startup TypeSafe introduced Jev, part of a new category it calls "System One Models" — built for fast, structured decisions rather than open-ended reasoning, and constrained to pick only from preset outputs so it technically cannot hallucinate. Jev matches existing large language models on straightforward tasks while running roughly two orders of magnitude faster and cheaper, and is now in early access.
