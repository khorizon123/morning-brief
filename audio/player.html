Russia's Deadliest Kyiv Strikes Yet as Air Defenses Run Dry

Russian ballistic missiles killed at least 15-17 people and injured dozens in Kyiv, damaging homes, a school and a children's hospital. Moscow skipped its usual drone barrage and went straight to ballistic missiles, a sign it's grown more confident as Ukraine's Patriot interceptor stocks dwindle. Grocery shelves in Kyiv are going bare for the first time since 2022 after Russia hit food warehouses, and roughly 40,000 residents slept in the subway system overnight.

Ukraine relies on Western-supplied Patriot missiles to stop incoming ballistic missiles; supplies have fallen to a third of last year's levels as allies husband their own stockpiles amid conflicts elsewhere.

US National Debt Tops $40 Trillion, Bond Yields Whipsaw

America's gross federal debt has surpassed $40 trillion, growing $3 trillion in a year. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent tried to calm the bond market this week by doubling long-term debt buybacks, and hinted more may come, but 30-year yields — which briefly hit 5.34%, the highest since 2007 — crept back up anyway. Bessent brushed off the volatility as '24-hour noise,' but critics say the intervention looks timed to the 2028 election rather than genuine price stability.

When yields rise, it costs the government (and everyone else) more to borrow; a Treasury buyback is an unusual attempt to prop up bond prices and hold yields down directly rather than through Fed policy.

Canada's trade minister Dominic LeBlanc said Ottawa and Washington are 'very close' to a deal that would halve steel and aluminum tariffs and cut auto duties, following a roughly three-hour meeting with US trade officials. Trump has repeatedly threatened steep tariffs on Canadian goods over trade and alcohol-stocking disputes, then pulled back at the last minute — a pattern critics call 'TACO' (Trump Always Chickens Out).

Walmart's Weakest Sales Growth in Six Years Rattles Wall Street

Walmart posted just 2.6% US comparable sales growth for the quarter, its smallest increase since 2020, even as e-commerce sales jumped 24% and fast delivery (under 30 minutes) doubled year-over-year. The retailer blamed lower drug prices from new federal regulations, and its stock fell almost 10% on the news. Combined with a resumed bond selloff, the report deepened worries that US consumer spending — the engine of economic growth — is softening.

Toyota Closes In on Ending GM's Century-Long US Sales Reign

Toyota is rapidly narrowing the sales gap with General Motors, threatening to end GM's roughly 100-year run atop the US auto market. GM has deliberately shrunk output — killing traditional sedans and cutting factory workers — to protect margins, with capacity utilization down to 73% from 78.5% in 2024, while tariffs cost it $3.1bn last year. Toyota, by contrast, is expanding US production in Texas and Kentucky and riding surging hybrid demand.

Stripe's $8bn OpenRouter Buy Is About AI Safety Data, Not Just Routing

Stripe's roughly $8bn acquisition of OpenRouter — a marketplace that routes developer requests across AI models by price and performance — pays $1.5bn to founders and $6bn to investors, versus a $1.3bn valuation just three months ago. Analysts argue the real prize is OpenRouter's view into more than 10 trillion tokens processed daily, giving Stripe unmatched behavioral data for AI security and positioning it as a neutral safety broker in the AI ecosystem. It's also the clearest sign yet that AI's boom is entering an acquisitions phase: strategic buyers have spent $50.5bn on AI 'unicorns' this year excluding SpaceX's $250bn xAI deal, versus $18.9bn all of last year.

Slack Launches Shared Channels Where AI Agents Write Code

Slack rolled out Slack Code, letting teams spin up 'code channels' where AI agents (ChatGPT, Claude, Devin, Vercel, GitHub) write software live while engineers and non-engineers watch and steer from within Slack. Deploys require human approval, and finished projects leave behind a searchable archived channel. It's Slack's bid to own the collaborative workspace where human and AI coworkers meet, rather than compete directly on building the best coding agent.

Anthropic's Supervoting Stock Plan Could Complicate Its Record IPO

Ahead of a potential IPO valuing it near $2 trillion, Anthropic plans to give all seven co-founders — including CEO Dario Amodei and his sister, president Daniela Amodei — supervoting shares with outsized voting power, an unusually broad arrangement compared to peers like Google or Meta where one or two people hold control. Company

OpenRouter

OpenRouter runs a marketplace that lets developers route AI requests across hundreds of models — from OpenAI to DeepSeek to Anthropic — automatically picking whichever is cheapest or fastest for a given task, so apps aren't locked into a single AI provider. It just became Stripe's biggest-ever acquisition at roughly $8 billion, up from a $1.3 billion valuation only three months earlier. The surprising twist: what made it so valuable wasn't the routing business itself but the firehose of data behind it — OpenRouter reportedly processes more than 10 trillion tokens a day, giving whoever owns it an unmatched, cross-network view into how AI models actually get used (and misused) in the wild. That data trove is why analysts think Stripe's real motive is AI safety and security infrastructure, not just payments.
