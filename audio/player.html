Iran Signals Hormuz Will Stay Shut Until US 'Changes Behaviour'

Iran's new Supreme National Security Council chief said the Strait of Hormuz won't reopen until Washington meets Tehran's conditions, even as US forces fired on a Panama-flagged ship for violating the American blockade of Iranian ports. Iran is meanwhile becoming what analysts call a 'survival economy' — rationing goods, restricting foreign currency and shifting economic pain onto households to outlast US pressure without folding at the negotiating table.

The Strait of Hormuz carries about a fifth of the world's oil and LNG; its closure since the US-Iran war escalated has kept crude prices elevated and given Tehran outsized leverage over Gulf neighbors even as its own oil exports have stalled.

Trump Was Secretly Whisked Off Air Force One Via Catering Truck After Iran Threat

After last month's NATO summit in Turkey, Trump publicly boarded Air Force One in front of cameras but was actually smuggled off via an airport catering container and flown out on a separate military jet, after intelligence — reportedly from Israel — warned of a credible Iranian shoulder-to-air missile threat against the president. The original Air Force One flew on anyway with reporters, staff and military personnel aboard as unwitting decoys. Chuck Schumer is now demanding Congress be briefed on the operation.

It echoes a century-old pattern of presidents obscuring their movements for security (Bush's secret Baghdad trip, Clinton's decoy flight to South Asia), but this is believed to be the first time such a full decoy operation was used during Trump's presidency.

Colombia Earthquake Death Toll Passes 250 as Rescue Continues

The death toll from Monday's 7.4-magnitude earthquake in western Colombia has climbed past 250, with more than 500 injured and over 1,575 buildings damaged in Cali, Pereira and Quibdó. New president Abelardo de la Espriella, just days into office, declared a national disaster and dispatched his cabinet to the hardest-hit regions; the Trump administration has pledged $15.5 million in relief.

The quake struck the same region hit by a similarly powerful pair of tremors that killed thousands in neighboring Venezuela in June, raising fears of comparable devastation.

JD Vance Asked Ukraine to Halt Strikes on Tankers Carrying Kazakh Oil via Russian Port

Vance asked Zelenskyy in a July 31 call to stop Ukrainian drone strikes on tankers loading at Russia's Novorossiysk port, alarmed that the attacks were destabilizing oil markets and hurting US energy majors Chevron and Exxon, both of which hold stakes in the Kazakh pipeline consortium that uses the terminal. Ukraine has complied, halting the strikes since. Brent crude has traded near $89 a barrel, sharply above pre-war levels.

Kazakh crude via Novorossiysk has become a critical substitute for Middle Eastern supply constrained by the ongoing Iran conflict and Hormuz blockade, making it a rare point of overlapping interest between US energy firms and Russia's export infrastructure.

Nvidia Enlists Wall Street Giants for $500bn AI Financing Push

Nvidia is teaming with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to assemble more than $500 billion in 'compute financing platforms,' effectively shifting systemic risk for the AI buildout from Nvidia's balance sheet onto Wall Street investors. BlackRock's Larry Fink called raising the money 'as fast as possible' urgent, and firms are exploring loans, credit and securitized 'yield-based products.' The move comes as Morgan Stanley projects hyperscalers will spend $3.5 trillion on AI infrastructure through 2028, and as Nvidia's own stock fell after the financing news broke, reflecting investor unease about leverage building up across the AI economy.

AstraZeneca-BMS $400bn Megamerger Collapses After Investor Revolt

Talks for what would have been one of the biggest pharma deals ever fell apart within a day of the FT reporting them: AstraZeneca shares dropped 9% on the news, its worst day since the pandemic, prompting the board to terminate discussions that evening. The proposed mostly share-based deal would have seen AstraZeneca buy Bristol Myers Squibb at a premium, but investors balked at BMS's looming patent cliff and UK officials worried about a British industrial crown jewel shifting its center of gravity to the US.

Refining Margins Surge as Iran War and Ukrainian Strikes Squeeze Fuel Supply

The US 3-2-1 crack spread — a benchmark for turning crude into gasoline and diesel — jumped above $70 last month versus a typical range in the teens, as Iran war disruptions, Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and Chinese fuel-export curbs pull refined products off the market. The IEA now expects global oil demand to fall 1.6 million barrels a day in 2026, deepening a supply squeeze even as Gulf refinery repairs from Iranian strikes are expected to take months.

Anthropic to Watermark All Claude-Generated Text and Code

Anthropic detailed plans to embed invisible, EU AI Act-compliant watermarks into text, code and file outputs from Claude that persist even when copied elsewhere, with files also getting the industry-standard C2PA provenance label. New models shipping after August 2 will have the feature built in, with older models retrofitted later; Anthropic will also release its own detection tools. The move — which drew mixed reactions from users worried about surveillance — comes as Anthropic separately courts investors ahead of a targeted September/October IPO, seeking to reassure them about competition from cheaper Chinese models and political backlash to data centers.

Meta's Zuckerberg Calls Open-Weight Models 'Open Source' — Critics Say That's Misleading

Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model for always-on local agents, alongside a 6,500-word essay from Mark Zuckerberg arguing 'open source AI' is a national-security imperative. But unlike true open-source software, open-weight models don't disclose training code or data — users can tweak the model's weights (the numerical values shaping its behavior) but can't retrain or fully inspect it, leaving Meta uniquely positioned to benefit from models it alone knows how to build and afford to run at scale.

Anthropic's Claude Improves Riemann Hypothesis Bound From 41.6% to 67.2%

An unreleased Claude model, coordinating multiple subagents to run numerical checks after attempting roughly 650 ideas, pushed forward the lower bound of zeros satisfying the Riemann hypothesis — one of math's most famous unsolved problems — from 41.6% to 67.2%. Two mathematicians and a formal validation process confirmed the result, adding to a string of recent frontier-model breakthroughs on long-standing open problems.

The release follows a summer of frontier labs disclosing their own models crossing critical cybersecurity risk thresholds, underscoring how seriously AI safety teams are now treating hacking-capable systems.

Suspected Chinese hackers used publicly available open-source AI agents to build an autonomous hacking tool that ran coordinated reconnaissance and intrusions against Taiwanese government websites, in what an Israeli AI security firm called a first-of-its-kind breach. Company

River AI

Founded two months ago by former xAI co-founder Igor Babuschkin, River AI just raised $1.1 billion to build open-source AI that's trained, tuned and run by individuals rather than big AI labs — a direct rebuke of the Anthropic/OpenAI/Google model of closed, centrally-controlled systems. Its live API reportedly turns open-weight models into ones a business can genuinely own, compressing what's normally a lengthy fine-tuning process into minutes. Babuschkin, who previously worked at OpenAI, Tesla and Google before helping Musk found xAI in 2023, says the goal is a highly customizable personal assistant that runs on a user's own private hardware across devices. The surprising fact: despite having almost nothing to show publicly yet, River commanded a $1.1bn raise purely on Babuschkin's pedigree and the growing appetite for alternatives to Big AI's watermarking and access controls.
