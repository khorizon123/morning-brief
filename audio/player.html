AI Slowdown Call Triggers Global Backlash and Market Jitters

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging AI labs to deliberately 'pace' development of frontier models, warning that a swarm of agents could threaten to take over the internet within 6-12 months. Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Demis Hassabis and Satya Nadella all voiced support, but Trump dismissed the concerns as "negative forces," and China's Ministry of State Security issued its own warning that AI poses risks to Beijing's political and social security. Tech stocks fell sharply in Asia and Europe on the news, with SoftBank down as much as 13%.

Amodei's essay comes as Anthropic prepares a blockbuster IPO, and skeptics note the timing could serve as marketing for the 'safe AI' player even as it complicates the antitrust optics of rival labs coordinating together.

Company

Open Cosmos

Open Cosmos is a British satellite maker and Earth-observation data provider that just raised €300 million ($346 million), pushing its valuation to roughly $2 billion. Founded 11 years ago, the company sells governments and businesses early-warning data on floods, wildfires and other climate disasters gathered from its own low-Earth-orbit satellites — bookings hit about $175 million last year. It's now expanding into orbital broadband and IoT connectivity, putting it in more direct competition with SpaceX's Starlink. The surprising fact: despite being a relatively small player, Open Cosmos is part of a European space-tech funding wave that's nearly doubling year over year, as the bloc scrambles to reduce its dependence on Musk's satellite infrastructure.
