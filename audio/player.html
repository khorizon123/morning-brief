Nepal-Tibet Flash Flood Toll Nears 300, Over 1,000 Missing

A glacier collapse triggered a catastrophic flash flood on the Nepal-Tibet border, killing at least 273 people with more than 1,000 still missing, including dozens of foreign tourists and Indian pilgrims. Chinese authorities have warned of further landslide risk as rescue teams from Nepal and China continue searching devastated river valleys.

Scientists believe a large ice chunk broke off a glacier in the Himalayas, unleashing a torrent equivalent in force to a magnitude-4.4 to 5.2 earthquake — a disaster researchers link to warming-driven glacial instability.

Meta to Pay Up to $17.1bn, Overhaul Instagram and Facebook for Teens

Meta settled with 47 states and territories over claims it designed Facebook and Instagram to be addictive and harmful to children, agreeing to pay up to $17.1bn and impose sweeping product changes: two-hour daily limits for under-18s, no access from midnight to 6am, removal of 'like' counts and beauty filters, and new anti-stranger-contact protections. Meta will only pay the full penalty if rivals TikTok, YouTube and Snap adopt similar restrictions — they've so far agreed to just $12bn combined.

The case echoes the 1990s tobacco litigation in scale and intent, aiming to force an industry-wide standard rather than punish one company; effectiveness hinges on unproven age-verification technology.

CIA Director Ratcliffe's Rare Moscow Visit Raises Eyebrows

John Ratcliffe spent hours in Moscow this week — the first known visit by a sitting US spy chief to Russia since the 2022 Ukraine invasion — for talks whose purpose remains undisclosed. The trip comes as Washington also pushes a sweeping new sanctions regime against Iran and continues tariff brinkmanship with Canada.

Nvidia Posts Record $96bn Quarter, Guides for 70% Sales Growth

Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue of $96.2bn and roughly $60bn in profit — both more than double a year earlier — beating Wall Street forecasts for a 15th straight quarter. CFO Colette Kress said sales should grow another 70% next year, constrained by supply rather than demand, calming fears of an AI bubble. Shares jumped as much as 7% in after-hours and pre-market trading, lifting chipmakers like Micron, Marvell and Broadcom alongside storage names Sandisk and Western Digital. Salesforce also posted an upbeat quarter, crediting AI and a $2.6bn gain on its Anthropic stake for an 11% revenue rise.

Wall Street Banks Look to Offload AI Data-Center Risk

Lenders including JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley and SMBC are exploring 'synthetic risk transfers' and special-purpose vehicles to shift exposure to the riskiest AI data-center loans off their balance sheets, and onto retail investors via pensions and insurance products. Interest in data-center debt is cooling as some contracts let Big Tech avoid fully repaying loans by lease-end, forcing banks to reassess long-term AI compute demand. The retreat comes even as public opposition to data centers grows — a March Gallup poll found 71% of Americans oppose one being built nearby, more than oppose a nuclear plant.

OpenAI's In-House Chip Jalapeño Moves Toward Deployment

OpenAI released early benchmarks for Jalapeño, its custom inference chip built for low-latency agent workloads, showing higher throughput per kilowatt and lower token latency than commercial systems tested on GPT-OSS 120B. OpenAI plans to deploy Jalapeño in its own infrastructure by year-end; AI reportedly helped design the chip's circuits and program its kernels. It's part of a broader race — alongside Google's TPUs and Amazon's Trainium — for AI labs to reduce dependence on Nvidia.

Company

Emerald AI

Emerald AI is a two-year-old startup whose software helps data centers dynamically flex their power draw to ease strain on electrical grids during peak demand — a growing problem as AI compute buildout collides with grid capacity limits. It recently raised $150 million at a $1.05 billion valuation led by Energize Capital, arriving just as public opposition to new data centers (71% of Americans, per Gallup) and bank nervousness about data-center debt are both climbing. Surprisingly, the company's technology doesn't require new hardware — it works by software-throttling compute jobs in real time so facilities can act as flexible grid assets rather than fixed loads. That positions it as a rare AI-adjacent business benefiting from, rather than contributing to, the backlash against power-hungry data centers.
