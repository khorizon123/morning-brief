Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard Lets AI Agents Run Lab Equipment

Anthropic introduced the Model Hardware Standard, a research preview that lets AI agents look up, learn and directly operate physical machines — microscopes, robotic arms, assembly-line gear — without custom-coded integrations, mirroring how its Model Context Protocol standardized software connections. Setup drops from weeks to 'hours or minutes,' the company claims; in one test, Claude taught itself to align a laser through trial and error and then automated the routine. Tecan, QIAGEN and AWS are partnering, with Hugging Face and Raspberry Pi planning to add it to their device lines.

Company

Cognition

Cognition is the three-year-old maker of Devin, an autonomous coding agent designed to manage entire software projects rather than just autocomplete lines, and it positions itself as the independent alternative to giants like Anthropic and OpenAI by letting customers plug in rival AI models instead of only its own. Its client roster includes Goldman Sachs, Anduril and Mercedes-Benz, and it recently absorbed the remains of Windsurf after Google poached that startup's top engineers for $2.4bn. The company is now raising fresh capital at a $46bn valuation, nearly double its mark from three months ago, as its annualized revenue has roughly doubled to over $800m. Surprisingly, in a field dominated by giant frontier labs, Cognition's whole pitch is model-agnosticism — betting that customers want the best agent, not the best in-house model.
