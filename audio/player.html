Trump Threatens to Bomb Oman as Hormuz Standoff Passes 60-Day Deadline

Trump said during a Fox News interview that the US would 'bomb the shit out of' Oman if the Gulf state 'gets in the way' of Washington's efforts to strike a deal with Iran, even as the 60-day ceasefire deadline lapsed. Oman has been trying to broker terms to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but US officials increasingly view its mediation as hostile. Shipping through the strait remains a trickle, and oil prices closed above $90 a barrel for the first time in two weeks.

Oman has long been Washington's chosen back-channel to Tehran; threatening to bomb a mediating ally marks an escalation even by the standards of this monthslong standoff. The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global oil supply, so any disruption there ripples through energy markets worldwide.

Trump Orders Reduced US-South Korea Drills, Rattling Seoul

Trump directed the Pentagon to 'substantially reduce' the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises with South Korea, citing his 'very good relationship' with Kim Jong Un, and said Kim had responded positively to his outreach. The decision, coming five weeks before Xi Jinping's expected Washington visit, has deepened doubts among Indo-Pacific allies about US reliability, especially as the Pentagon simultaneously pulls ships from the region toward the Middle East.

The US and South Korea have run large joint exercises for decades to deter North Korea; scaling them back without reciprocal concessions echoes Trump's first-term summit diplomacy with Kim, which ultimately yielded no disarmament and let North Korea keep building weapons.

Kushner Meets Netanyahu After Hamas Talks in Push to Revive Gaza Deal

Jared Kushner met with Israeli PM Benjamin Netanyahu in Jerusalem, a day after meeting Hamas leaders in Egypt, in a fresh attempt to break the deadlock over Trump's 15-point Gaza peace plan, which Netanyahu had publicly rejected weeks earlier. Netanyahu's office called the talks 'deep and constructive' and said two working groups would be formed, on disarming militants in Gaza and public health in the territory.

Israel currently controls roughly 60% of the Gaza Strip amid a fragile ceasefire; Kushner has served as Trump's chief back-channel envoy to both Israeli and Hamas leadership throughout the on-and-off negotiations.

US-Canada Tariff Deadline Comes Down to the Wire

Canadian PM Mark Carney is racing to reach a deal with Trump before the US imposes fresh 50% tariffs on roughly $20bn of Canadian goods at midnight Wednesday. Talks have made some progress, with Washington pushing Canadian provinces to restock American alcohol and Canada seeking relief on lumber, cars and dairy protections, but negotiators expect the standoff to run to the final hours.

This tariff threat comes on top of existing US levies of up to 50% that have already hit Canada's auto, aluminum and wood sectors, part of a broader Trump-era trade squeeze on America's neighbors.

Global Bond Sell-Off Deepens as 30-Year Treasury Yield Hits 5.33%

Long-term government borrowing costs across major economies hit multi-decade highs, with the 30-year US Treasury yield rising to 5.33% (highest since 2007) and UK, German and French long bonds similarly at multi-decade or multi-year peaks. The moves reflect mounting worry about ballooning US debt (nearing $40tn), persistent inflation, and heavy AI-related bond issuance, compounded by Brent crude closing above $90 a barrel for the first time in two weeks amid the Iran war. China is the outlier, with its 10-year yield falling to a one-year low as weak growth pushes investors into sovereign debt. The renewed sell-off dragged US equity futures lower.

Mark Walter's Insurance Empire Under Federal Investigation, Rattling Private Credit

Federal prosecutors are examining whether financier Mark Walter — the Guggenheim Partners co-founder who parlayed a career in commercial paper into control of major insurers and, more recently, ownership stakes in the Dodgers and Lakers — improperly funneled insurance policyholder money into undisclosed related-party loans. Two of his insurers under holding company TWG disclosed in June that they'd failed to flag more than $20bn in related-party assets. Because Walter pioneered the now-widespread Wall Street strategy of buying life insurers and rotating their assets into illiquid private loans (since copied by Apollo, KKR and Brookfield), the scandal is stoking fears about how trillions in US retirement savings are managed across the private capital industry.

Cursor Launches GitHub Rival 'Origin' the Same Day GitHub Suffers Major Outage

Cursor, now part of SpaceX after an acquisition to tap its GPU resources, launched Origin, a beta code-hosting platform that pairs every repository with Cursor's built-in coding agent and review tools, letting users mirror GitHub repos live across both platforms. The timing was extraordinary: GitHub suffered its second major outage this month, with degraded service for over six hours, just as Origin went live. Origin opens first to Cursor's paying customers, with heavier agent-native features coming soon. It puts Microsoft's two-decade lock on code hosting under the same AI-driven pressure that's already reshaping coding itself.

ByteDance and Hollywood's MPA Strike AI Video Copyright Deal

ByteDance agreed to a formal framework with the Motion Picture Association to bake film and TV copyright protections into its Seedance and Seedream video-generation models, months after a viral deepfake-style clip of Tom Cruise sparked the MPA's first-ever cease-and-desist against a major AI company. The guardrails will apply across every app running the models, including CapCut, Dreamina and TikTok's US spinoff. It's a notable concession from a Chinese AI lab, though rivals like Kling and Alibaba's Wan models mean the MPA's fight is far from over.
