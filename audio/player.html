Manchester City Found Guilty on 114 of 115 Financial Fair Play Charges

An independent Premier League panel found Manchester City guilty of breaching the league's financial rules in numerous ways over several years — 114 of 115 charges. Punishments haven't been decided and City is expected to appeal, so the fallout could take months or years to play out.

Manchester City has faced allegations since 2018 of disguising owner investment as sponsorship revenue to skirt spending controls; a guilty verdict threatens fines, points deductions, or even expulsion from England's top league.

A federal appeals court ruled that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had the legal authority to designate Anthropic a supply-chain security risk, after the company refused to let its AI be used for mass surveillance or autonomous weapons. It's a win for the Trump administration in its ongoing feud with one of the top AI labs.

The ruling adds a legal dimension to the deepening rift between Washington and Anthropic over military AI use, just as frontier labs face scrutiny for AI safety incidents.

Supreme Court Allows Trump Administration's Voter-Citizenship Database

The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that the administration can deploy a national database using citizenship data and Social Security numbers to help states screen voter rolls. Federal law's 90-day pre-election quiet period means large-scale removals are unlikely before the midterms, and the court's three liberal justices dissented, warning eligible citizens could be wrongly flagged.

Trump has pushed unproven claims that noncitizens are voting in significant numbers; critics say past uses of similar databases have mistakenly flagged citizens.

US Long-Term Bond Yields Hit Highest Since 2004 in Fresh Sell-Off

A resumed sell-off in US government debt pushed long-term borrowing costs to their highest level since 2004, with the 10-year yield hovering near 5% and the 30-year at multi-decade highs. Investors cite a weak five-year note auction, hawkish Fed commentary, heavy federal issuance and stalled progress on lowering the deficit. The rout has spread to 30-year mortgage rates, which topped 7% for the first time in nearly two years, and analysts warn it could stress other corners of the financial system.

SoftBank sold roughly $11bn of dollar- and euro-denominated bonds — one of the largest high-yield debt deals ever — some tranches at the steepest yields the company has offered, to bankroll its multibillion-dollar investment in OpenAI. Company

Nscale

Nscale is a British AI cloud provider racing toward a $35bn New York listing to fund the data centers it's building to supply computing power to Anthropic and Microsoft under the Stargate initiative. It's become a poster child for the scramble to finance the physical infrastructure behind the AI boom, tapping public markets rather than waiting on venture rounds. Surprisingly, despite touting its Microsoft and Anthropic contracts prominently in its S-1 filing, Nscale has downplayed that ByteDance — the Chinese owner of TikTok — was actually one of its biggest early customers, an omission that's drawing scrutiny given ongoing US-China tech tensions. The company's rapid rise illustrates how compute-supply middlemen have become as consequential to the AI race as the model developers themselves.
