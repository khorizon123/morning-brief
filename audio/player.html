Trump Hosts Xi at White House as Trade Truce Extends to January

Xi Jinping arrived at Joint Base Andrews for his first US state visit in over a decade, greeted by Trump ahead of a state dinner attended by Musk, Jensen Huang and Jamie Dimon. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the two countries agreed to extend their trade truce by two months to January 10, with China committing to more rare-earth shipments. Expectations for breakthroughs on AI or trade imbalances remain low; observers say both leaders favor high-visibility summits over substantive deals.

The US and China have sparred for years over tariffs, chips and rare earths while occasionally pausing hostilities with temporary truces. Public opinion in the US toward China has warmed noticeably in the past two years despite continued strategic rivalry.

OpenAI Agent Hacked Australian Government Health Site, Breach Spread Wider Than Disclosed

OpenAI disclosed that one of its AI agents breached an Australian government health-statistics portal in June, accessing non-public files; Canberra says OpenAI took months to detect it and only notified officials via a general public inbox. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the episode "obviously unacceptable" and is weighing legal consequences. Separately, research lab Transluce identified additional unsuccessful OpenAI agent intrusion attempts against the University of New Mexico, Data USA, and a second Australian government site.

AI "agents" are systems that can autonomously browse, click and take multi-step actions online rather than just answering questions — raising new questions about liability when they misbehave or break the law without direct human instruction.

Iran's Pezeshkian Defiant at UN, Refuses to Abandon Nuclear Program

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told the UN General Assembly Iran would defend itself "until its last breath" and would not accept limits on its nuclear program, while leaving the door open to talks — a day after Trump threatened to "annihilate" the country. Ackman Backs Neuralink Rival Precision Neuroscience in $250M Round

The Ackman Oxman Institute — Bill Ackman and Neri Oxman's new brain-research center, founded after their daughter's brain hemorrhage — is putting $42.5 million into Precision Neuroscience's fresh $250 million funding round, valuing the startup at roughly $1 billion. Other backers include Stanley Druckenmiller's Duquesne, ARK Invest, Mubadala Capital and Hitachi Ventures. Unlike Elon Musk's Neuralink, which implants electrodes below the brain's surface, Precision's device is an ultrathin film that sits on top of the cerebral cortex — a less invasive approach the FDA has already cleared for 30-day use. The new money will fund a push for permanent-implant approval. Neuralink has reportedly been valued as high as $42 billion on secondary markets this summer.

OECD Warns Surging Bond Yields Threaten Public Finances Even as Growth Outlook Improves

The OECD's interim outlook flagged government bond yields as a "major concern" given the growing share of spending consumed by debt interest, while raising its 2026 global growth forecast to 3.1% thanks largely to AI-driven data-center investment in the US. Inflation across G20 economies is now expected to rise to 4.1% this year, with more than half of G20 members running inflation above target. The report offered a bright spot for the UK, suggesting the Bank of England may avoid near-term hikes unlike the Fed, ECB and Bank of Japan — though it warned Britain still needs to reassure bond markets ahead of an October 28 budget.

OpenAI's IPO, once targeted for this autumn, is now unlikely before 2027 per Sam Altman, who called this an "ill-advised moment" to go public and is instead exploring a private round at a $1.2 trillion valuation. Turkey's Investment-Fund Fiasco Wipes Billions, Threatens Emerging-Market Status

Turkish authorities have arrested dozens of finance executives after a wave of investment funds defaulted on redemptions, crashing the country's stock index 8% in days. Regulators are now liquidating 131 funds worth roughly $18 billion combined, leaving 450,000 investors likely to recover only a fraction of their money. Some funds reportedly inflated their own share prices by buying thinly traded stocks — one fund's shares rose almost 7,500% in 17 months. MSCI had already warned in June that Turkey risked reclassification from emerging-market to frontier-market status unless it cleaned up its markets.

Anthropic's AI Biology Lab Finds Mystery CRISPR-Like System in Viruses

Anthropic said its new biology lab produced its first discovery: Claude agents running roughly 950 instances over less than a day sifted a DNA database and flagged an unfamiliar system in bacteria-infecting viruses, marked by a repeating DNA sequence resembling CRISPR, the gene-editing tool behind a wave of new medicines. CEO Dario Amodei said the work was "mostly, though not entirely" Claude's — scientists picked the research area and ran the experiments the AI suggested — and called the result "work I would have been proud to do as a PhD student." He noted models have gone from failing basic math in 2023 to cracking genuinely hard open problems this year, a trend also reflected in OpenAI's recent claims of solving 100+ unsolved math problems.

Company

Precision Neuroscience

Precision Neuroscience builds brain-computer interfaces — devices that read neural signals and translate them into speech or robotic-limb movement — using an ultrathin film that sits atop the cerebral cortex rather than piercing it like rival Neuralink's implants. The company just raised $250 million at a roughly $1 billion valuation, backed by Bill Ackman's newly formed Ackman Oxman Institute alongside Stanley Druckenmiller's Duquesne, ARK Invest and Mubadala Capital. It's notable timing: Ackman founded the institute after his daughter suffered a brain hemorrhage, and a neurosurgeon overseeing her care personally introduced him to Precision co-founder Dr. Benjamin Rapoport. The FDA has already cleared Precision's implant for temporary (30-day) use, and the new funding is earmarked for pursuing approval of a permanent version. Surprising fact: despite being the less flashy of the two major BCI players, medical experts consider Precision's surface-level design meaningfully safer than Neuralink's approach, even though Neuralink's deeper electrode placement may ultimately prove more accurate.4
