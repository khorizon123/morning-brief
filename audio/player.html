Spain Deploys Troops to Ceuta as Enclave 'Vents' Over Migrant Surge

Spain has begun rounding up and repatriating Moroccan migrants who crossed into Ceuta hoping to stay, as soldiers patrol the North African enclave amid vocal local opposition. EU home affairs ministers are set to meet Tuesday to discuss the crisis after Spanish PM Sánchez pushed back against criticism of his handling of the surge.

Ceuta is a small Spanish territory on Morocco's coast that has become a flashpoint for African migration into Europe; this week saw a record daily influx of tens of thousands of people, dwarfing the 2015 migration crisis peak.

Ares in Talks, Intesa Launches Hostile €35bn Bid for World's Oldest Bank

Italy's Banca Intesa, the country's biggest lender, has made a hostile 35-billion-euro ($40 billion) cash-and-shares bid for Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena, the 1472-founded lender that has stumbled through derivatives scandals and a taxpayer bailout since 2008. Monte dei Paschi's CEO Luigi Lovaglio is scrambling for alternatives, including possibly reviving stalled merger talks with smaller rival Banco BPM, while facing backlash from the city of Siena and the Catholic Church. Analysts say the standoff could set a template for how Europe handles bank consolidation going forward.

SpaceX Leads a New Land Rush — for Radio Spectrum, Not Land

SpaceX and rivals are racing to lock up radio spectrum, the invisible airwaves that carry satellite and wireless signals, in a scramble likened to the 19th-century land rushes. In the US, the FCC treats spectrum licenses as near-property rights, and the industry has sunk over $230 billion into FCC auctions and $700 billion into networks; Starlink alone accounts for roughly two-thirds of active satellites in orbit and about 60% of SpaceX's revenue. The fight is going global too: China and other countries are pushing for 'equitable access' to satellite frequencies at the UN's International Telecommunication Union, setting up a showdown at the 2027 World Radiocommunication Conference in Shanghai. SpaceX flagged the regulatory risk directly in its $1.8 trillion IPO prospectus, warning some countries may start favoring homegrown satellite operators over first-mover American ones.

Company

Simile

Simile is a year-old, Stanford-spinout AI startup that builds 'agentic twins' — AI agents modeled to simulate how specific groups of real people, from equity analysts to consumer segments, would react to a new product, ad campaign, or brand message. Instead of running traditional focus groups or surveys, companies can poll these synthetic personas for faster, cheaper market research. The company just raised $200 million led by Greenoaks at a $2 billion valuation, a striking jump for a business barely a year old. The obvious catch — and the question investors are most focused on — is how closely these AI-simulated opinions actually track real human responses, an accuracy gap the company hasn't fully disclosed.
