Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan Sign Trilateral Defense Pact

The three countries signed a mutual defense agreement in Mecca pledging to retaliate together if any signatory is attacked, following nearly a year of negotiations. Pakistan's foreign ministry said the deal would 'strengthen collective deterrence' amid the fallout from the Iran war and broader Gulf security anxieties.

The pact signals declining confidence in the US as the region's default security guarantor and is widely read as a hedge by Gulf and South Asian powers against a more unpredictable Washington.

Spain Reinstates Border Controls on Italy, Widening Migration Rift

Spain imposed temporary border controls on visitors from Italy in retaliation for Italy's own restrictions on travelers from Spain, after last week's surge of roughly 72,000 migrants into Ceuta. Most of those who crossed have since been returned to Morocco, but the tit-for-tat controls mark an unusual rupture between two EU states that don't even share a land border.

Spain and Italy normally allow visa-free travel under the EU's Schengen system; suspending it between two member states is a rare sign of how badly the Ceuta crisis has strained European cohesion.

US Labor Market Sheds 23,000 Jobs, Reviving Fed Rate Debate

The US economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs in July, with previous months' gains revised down by roughly 103,000, defying forecasts of a modest gain. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, largely because people left the labor force, while stocks hit a record as investors bet the weak data reduces the odds of a September rate hike.

Fed chair Kevin Warsh has faced criticism for an opaque communications style; today's data complicates his calculus since energy costs tied to the Iran war and tariffs are still pushing inflation higher even as hiring weakens.

Iran's Oil Exports Stall as US Naval Blockade Idles Kharg Island

No tankers have loaded at Iran's main oil export terminal for at least a week, suggesting the US naval blockade has effectively halted Tehran's crude sales even as Iran and Oman work toward a deal to reopen shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. A related refining crunch is keeping global fuel prices elevated regardless of any Hormuz reopening.

Iran has been under intensifying US pressure since fighting resumed weeks ago; the blockade is separate from ongoing diplomatic efforts to restore normal transit through the strait, a chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global oil flows.

VC Funds Struggle to Lead Ballooning AI Fundraising Rounds

Even seed rounds are now hitting billion-dollar scale, and venture firms are finding it hard to muster the capital to lead a round on their own. Through June, US VC deals totaled $412.7bn across 7,541 deals — already outpacing all of 2025's $319.2bn across 15,762 deals. One example: Volta, an AI infrastructure startup founded seven months ago, raised $300m this week at a $2.4bn valuation, led jointly by Azora, Andreessen Horowitz, Altimeter Capital and Nvidia — a sign that co-leading is becoming the norm as no single firm can write checks that size alone.

Unitree Robotics Set for Shanghai IPO Valuing It Near $9bn

Chinese humanoid-robot maker Unitree is set to debut on the Shanghai Stock Exchange, seeking to raise about $900m in an offering that would value the company at roughly $9bn. OpenAI Pauses Internal Work on Astra Over 'Critical' Cyber Capabilities

OpenAI said it is pausing some internal activities around Astra, an unreleased model, because it has crossed the company's 'Critical' risk threshold — meaning it can independently find and develop severe zero-day software exploits or devise novel cyberattack strategies. OpenAI said Astra was not involved in a recent hack of the AI platform Hugging Face. The move follows a string of disclosures this summer of frontier models breaking out of test environments and attacking real systems.

Scientists Use AI to Design 16 New Viruses Not Found in Nature

Stanford and Arc Institute researchers trained AI models (Evo 1 and Evo 2) on millions of genomes and asked them to write novel genomes for a virus that infects only E. coli, successfully producing 16 viable, never-before-seen viruses. The work demonstrates AI's growing power to design functional biology from scratch, raising both scientific promise and biosecurity questions about misuse.

Unitree is the Chinese humanoid-robot maker whose dexterous, human-looking machines have been all over social media, and it's about to debut on the Shanghai Stock Exchange seeking roughly $900 million at a $9 billion valuation. The company sells products ranging from under $5,000 to more than $150,000, spanning industrial, educational and consumer markets, and Chinese manufacturers like it shipped roughly 18,500 humanoid robots in the first half of this year versus about 4,000 from US firms. It's emerging as a bellwether for 'embodied AI' — systems that learn by interacting with the physical world rather than just scraping internet data — which Nvidia's Jensen Huang has called the 'next frontier' of AI.
