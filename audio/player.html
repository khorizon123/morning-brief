Israeli Strikes Kill 11 in Lebanon, Two Months Into Truce

Israel's military struck targets in Lebanon, killing at least 11 people, describing the attack as a 'response to an action' by Hizbollah. The strikes come despite a ceasefire that has held, nominally, for two months.

Israel and Hizbollah agreed a truce late last year after over a year of cross-border fighting tied to the Gaza war, but Israel has continued periodic strikes it says target Hizbollah rearmament.

Colombia Earthquake Aftermath Reveals a Divided Country

New president Abelardo de la Espriella, three days into office when the 7.4-magnitude quake struck, is now confronting stark disparities in how wealthy and poor regions of Colombia are recovering from the disaster.

The earthquake, centered in western Colombia, has killed more than 250 people and damaged over 1,500 buildings in Cali, Pereira and Manizales; recovery is unfolding unevenly across the country's class lines.

Leipzig Drone Attack Exposes Gaps in Europe's Hybrid-Warfare Defenses

A drone carrying plastic explosives nearly destroyed a large transport plane at Leipzig/Halle Airport this month. Germany has not formally blamed Russia, but the near-miss is being read across Europe as a warning about vulnerability to Russian-style sabotage campaigns.

Hybrid warfare — sabotage, drone incursions and disinformation below the threshold of open conflict — has become a recurring feature of Russia's confrontation with NATO states since the invasion of Ukraine.

Kyiv's stockpile of Patriot interceptor missiles is dwindling just as Russia gears up for a winter bombardment campaign, putting Ukraine's recent military momentum at risk.

Patriot batteries, supplied mainly by the US and European allies, are Ukraine's primary shield against Russian ballistic missiles; a shortage would leave cities and infrastructure far more exposed heading into cold-weather months.

Bezos-Backed Group Buys 30%+ of Liverpool FC at $7bn-Plus Valuation

An investor group including Jeff Bezos, Amit Bhatia (son-in-law of steel billionaire Lakshmi Mittal) and Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin has agreed to buy over 30% of Liverpool FC from Fenway Sports Group, valuing the club above $7 billion. It's part of a wave of trophy sports-asset deals this month, following the record $12.5 billion Lakers sale to Bob Iger and Josh Kushner and Apollo's $2.6 billion Yankees stake. Sports franchises have become a favored parking spot for ultra-wealthy investors seeking scarce, appreciating assets with global brand power.

PayPal in Talks to Sell Itself to Stripe-Led Group

PayPal is negotiating a sale to a consortium including payments rival Stripe and private-equity firm Advent International, after rejecting an earlier joint offer of $60.50 a share as too low. A deal would be a striking reversal for PayPal, once the dominant name in online payments, now being circled by the newer fintech it helped inspire. Terms of a revised offer haven't been disclosed.

Singapore Bets on Open AI Access to Retain Finance Talent

Singapore's government and financial firms are betting that easy, unrestricted access to the latest AI models — a byproduct of the city-state's close ties to both the US and China — can stop finance professionals from decamping to Hong Kong. Unlike jurisdictions that restrict which foreign AI models firms may use, Singapore has positioned itself as neutral ground where banks and hedge funds can plug into whichever model performs best. The strategy reflects growing competition between Asian financial hubs to be the default base for AI-native trading and research teams.

Company

Oura

Oura makes a titanium smart ring that tracks sleep, heart rate variability and menstrual cycles, and has confidentially filed for an IPO as the wearables category — an industry worth roughly $44 billion globally — heats up. The Finnish-American company positions itself not as a fitness gadget but as a 'health intelligence platform,' and has already partnered with continuous-glucose-monitor maker Dexcom, which invested $75 million in 2024. Oura is notably litigious: it has sued seven competitors for patent infringement, winning three cases outright with four still pending. It now faces a crowded field that includes Apple, Samsung and Google (which bought Fitbit for $2.1 billion in 2021), all racing to embed themselves in the medical system as the ultimate long-term prize. Surprising fact: Oura's newest ring is thin enough to pass as ordinary jewelry, yet the company has aggressively litigated its way to winning three of seven patent lawsuits against rivals.
